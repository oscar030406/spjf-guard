Reading of the definitions (containers.py's docstring has the full rule):
  login_obs     only the logins whose next row in logins.log is a logout.  It
                covers 26.9% of the executed seconds, so it is a lower bound, not
                a measurement.
  login_act     every login, a censored close filled from the last event inside
                the window.  It covers 99.8% of the executed seconds, and it is
                the upper reading: when a student never logs out and comes back
                to the same session hours later, the container stays open for the
                whole stretch.  That is why login_act sits above proxy3600.
  proxy600/1800/3600   activity proxy at a 10 / 30 / 60 min idle gap; they cover
                100% of the executed seconds by construction.
  proxy1800_lg  the 30 min proxy with the login and logout instants added to the
                activity set.
  The primary number quoted below is proxy1800.

2  Shared pool: the smallest k that meets a deadline-window p99 wait target
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Wait = time from arrival to the start of service in a k-server non-preemptive
queue.  The statistic is the p99 over the 4,219,556 jobs that arrive within 24 h of an
assessment end -- the paper's own deadline window.  SPJF-M4 orders by the
development forward predictor; +G300 is the overtake-budget guard at B = 5 L.

policy       p99_dl at the paper's k        smallest k for p99_dl <=
             k=8      k=5      k=4           30 s     5 s     1 s
FCFS            22.17   109.71   263.12          8      11      14
SPJF-M4         11.98    44.69    66.57          7      10      13
SPJF+G300       13.31    69.93   217.56          7      10      13
SJF-ref          8.31    34.19    42.88          6       9      12

At every target the predictor saves one executor against FCFS, and the guard
costs nothing at the k where it matters (same k as ungated SPJF at all three
targets).  True SJF, which no scheduler can reach, saves one more.

4  Caveats that have to be stated with any of these numbers
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1) A dedicated container also serves the interactive IDE session, not only
     judging. Container-alive time is therefore an upper bound on what judging
     alone needs, and the utilisation below 0.2% is the utilisation of a
     container that is doing editing, reading and idling as well as running
     code. The comparison is between two architectures, not between two ways
     of running the same judge.

  2) A pooled executor still needs a sandbox start per job. C already includes
     interpreter start-up per the plan's field semantics (2.2), and the pool
     is charged the same C_cap the dedicated container was charged, so no
     start-up cost is omitted on either side -- but neither is the extra cost
     of a cold container that the dedicated design pays only once per login.

  3) Concurrency is counted from end-time logs at 1-second resolution. The log
     timestamp is the end of a run; the arrival is reconstructed as end - C.
     Two events one second apart cannot be separated, and a container's true
     creation and teardown instants are not recorded at all.

  4) logins.log is a per-user global history copied into every class
     directory: the same rows appear in several class-semesters and reach
     years outside the archive's own semester. They are deduplicated by (user,
     timestamp, kind) and clipped to the semester's event window. Only 11% to
     35% of logins have a logout written after them (per semester), so the
     observed close time is missing for most sessions; login_act fills those
     from activity and login_obs drops them.

  5) TEST blocks carry no execution time, so the time a container spends
     running a student's interactive test is counted as idle. Utilisation is a
     lower bound on 'container busy running code' for that reason, and the
     number of TEST blocks is 2.5 x the number of submissions in this pool.

  6) The overlay is a construction: the same six semesters replicated 44 times
     with week shifts, not 44 independent platforms. Both sides of every ratio
     are computed on that same construction, so the ratio is a property of the
     construction as much as of CodeBench.

  7) One overlay replicate (rep 0), no bootstrap interval. The paper's own
     tables carry week-block bootstrap intervals for the wait statistics; the
     k values here are point estimates on one trace.

5  What the paper can say, and what it cannot
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CAN (one sentence):

  On the same replayed demand, CodeBench's per-student design keeps a p99 of 1636
  containers alive at once (max 2725) and each container executes code for 0.12% of
  the time it is alive, whereas a shared non-preemptive pool holds the p99 wait of
  deadline-window jobs under 1 s with k = 14 executors under FCFS and k = 13 under
  the predictor-ordered policy -- a factor of 117 fewer executors and 24 x fewer
  provisioned executor-seconds per executed second.

CANNOT:

  - Cannot claim CodeBench ever queued: it has no shared queue, and nothing
    here measures a wait on the real platform. The pooled side is entirely
    simulated.

  - Cannot claim the dedicated containers are 24 x too large a provision for
    the platform as a whole -- they also run the IDE, which the pool does not
    replace.

  - Cannot claim a container count for the real single-course semesters that a
    reader may compare with a real deployment: the per-semester p99 is 10 to
    29 containers, and the large numbers here come from the 44-fold overlay.

  - Cannot claim the scheduler saves many executors: at every target tested it
    saves exactly one against FCFS (14 -> 13 at 1 s, 11 -> 10 at 5 s, 8 -> 7
    at 30 s). The saving from pooling is two orders of magnitude; the saving
    from ordering inside the pool is one executor.

  - Cannot claim a measured container lifetime: the platform writes a logout
    for only a minority of logins, so every alive-time number here is
    reconstructed, and the spread across the six definitions (p99 1003 to
    2525) is the honest uncertainty band.

  - Cannot report the ratio as a cost saving without a cost model: a container
    and an executor are not the same unit of hardware, and nothing here
    measures memory, CPU shares or price.
