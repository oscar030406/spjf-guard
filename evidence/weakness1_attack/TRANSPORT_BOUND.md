# A conditional transport bound for FCFS

## Finding

The proposed FCFS statement is true under its strong premises. For identical
servers, a common finite set of job identities, identical service requirements,
identical initial server availability, and one fixed FCFS rank that is respected by
both arrival vectors, the vector of FCFS start times is 1-Lipschitz in the sup norm
of the identity-matched arrival perturbation. Each job's waiting time is therefore
2-Lipschitz. A common-cohort mean and a standard linearly interpolated empirical p99
inherit the same 2-Lipschitz bound.

This result can bound the difference between shadow FCFS on a policy's realised
trace and an independently evolving FCFS baseline. It does not by itself bound the
open-loop-versus-closed-loop term in the existing counterexamples, because that term
changes the arrival vector under the predicted-size policy rather than under FCFS.
Non-preemptive SJF is not Lipschitz in this way, even when arrival rank is preserved.

## FCFS recursion and theorem

Label the common jobs in their fixed FCFS order as (1,\ldots,n). Let
(A=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)) and (A'=(a'_1,\ldots,a'_n)), where both vectors are
nondecreasing in this same labelled order. Exact timestamp ties are allowed only if
both systems use the same deterministic tie-break. Let the common nonnegative
service requirements be (C=(c_1,\ldots,c_n)).

Immediately before job (i), write the sorted vector of the (k) servers' absolute
availability times as

\[
  v_{i-1,1}\leq\cdots\leq v_{i-1,k}.
\]

Starting from a common (v_0), work-conserving FCFS has the recursion

\[
  s_i=\max\{a_i,v_{i-1,1}\},
  \qquad
  v_i=\operatorname{sort}
  \bigl(v_{i-1,2},\ldots,v_{i-1,k},s_i+c_i\bigr).
\]

Choosing the earliest-available server is without loss for identical servers. If
several servers are already idle, it merely fixes a harmless server-label
convention.

**Theorem.** If

\[
  \|A-A'\|_\infty\leq\epsilon,
\]

then, for every job (i),

\[
  |s_i-s'_i|\leq\epsilon,
  \qquad
  |w_i-w'_i|\leq 2\epsilon,
\]

where (w_i=s_i-a_i).

**Proof.** Sorting is non-expansive in the sup norm:

\[
  \|\operatorname{sort}(x)-\operatorname{sort}(y)\|_\infty
  \leq \|x-y\|_\infty.
\]

To see this, if every paired coordinate differs by at most (d), the (r)-th
order statistic of either vector cannot exceed the (r)-th order statistic of the
other by more than (d); applying the argument in both directions proves the
claim.

Suppose inductively that
(|v_{i-1}-v'_{i-1}|_\infty\leq\epsilon). Since the maximum of two scalars is
1-Lipschitz under the sup norm,

\[
  |s_i-s'_i|
  \leq
  \max\{|a_i-a'_i|,
         |v_{i-1,1}-v'_{i-1,1}|\}
  \leq\epsilon.
\]

Pair the retained server coordinates with one another and pair
(s_i+c_i) with (s'_i+c_i). Every coordinate of these two unsorted update
vectors differs by at most (epsilon); sorting preserves that bound. Thus
(|v_i-v'_i|_\infty\leq\epsilon). The common initial state supplies the base
case. Finally,

\[
  |w_i-w'_i|
  = |(s_i-s'_i)-(a_i-a'_i)|
  \leq 2\epsilon.
  \qquad\square
\]

The factor two for waits cannot generally be reduced. With one effective server,
move a long predecessor's arrival later by (epsilon) and the next job's arrival
earlier by (epsilon), while retaining their order. The predecessor's completion
moves later by (epsilon), so the next job's wait moves by (2epsilon). The same
construction embeds into any (k>1) system by keeping (k-1) other servers busy
with common long jobs.

## Means, p99, and a transported theorem reference

For a fixed common cohort (I), the per-job result immediately gives

\[
  \left|
  \frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{i\in I}w_i-
  \frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{i\in I}w'_i
  \right|\leq 2\epsilon.
\]

Sorting a finite wait vector is also non-expansive in the sup norm. Every empirical
order statistic therefore changes by at most (2\epsilon), and a linearly
interpolated p99, being a convex combination of two adjacent order statistics,
changes by at most (2\epsilon). The cohort must contain the same identities in
both systems. A policy-specific arrival-window cohort does not meet this condition.

Let (A^P) be the arrival vector generated when policy (P) controls the system,
and let (A^F) be the vector generated by an independently operated closed-loop
FCFS system. For a mean or p99 functional (Q) on a common cohort, define

\[
  B_{\rm shadow}
  =Q(W^F(A^P,C))-Q(W^P(A^P,C))
\]

and

\[
  B_{\rm independent}
  =Q(W^F(A^F,C))-Q(W^P(A^P,C)).
\]

If the common-identity, common-cost and common-rank premises hold and
(|A^P-A^F|_\infty\leq\epsilon), then

\[
  |B_{\rm independent}-B_{\rm shadow}|
  \leq 2\epsilon.
\]

Thus a positive shadow benefit (b) implies only the conditional sensitivity
statement

\[
  B_{\rm independent}\in[b-2\epsilon,b+2\epsilon].
\]

Its sign is robust under these premises when (b>2\epsilon). No value of
(epsilon) is identified by the present open-loop logs.

The same argument transports a per-job guard guarantee with extra slack. If the
sample-path theorem gives

\[
  W_i^P(A^P,C)\leq W_i^F(A^P,C)+G,
\]

then

\[
  W_i^P(A^P,C)\leq W_i^F(A^F,C)+G+2\epsilon.
\]

This remains a comparison across identity-matched waits whose own arrival instants
may differ. It is conditional sensitivity analysis, not an unconditional guarantee
against an independently evolving system.

## Why this does not bound the existing fixed-replay contrast

When the fixed open-loop trace is (A^F), the existing decomposition uses

\[
  B_{\rm open}=Q(W^F(A^F,C))-Q(W^P(A^F,C))
\]

and

\[
  B_{\rm closed}=Q(W^F(A^F,C))-Q(W^P(A^P,C)).
\]

Their difference is

\[
  B_{\rm closed}-B_{\rm open}
  =Q(W^P(A^F,C))-Q(W^P(A^P,C)).
\]

The FCFS theorem says nothing about this policy-(P) term. There is no analogous
arrival-Lipschitz result for non-preemptive SJF. For any (k), keep (k-1) servers
busy with common long blocker jobs. On the remaining server, let a unit job start at
time zero while a job of size (H) waits. Move a later unit job from
(1-\epsilon/2) to (1+\epsilon/2). Arrival rank is unchanged and the sup-norm
perturbation is (epsilon). In the first trace, the later unit job is present at
the time-1 dispatch and SJF serves it before the size-(H) job. In the second, the
long job starts at time 1 and non-preemptively blocks the later unit job for nearly
(H) time units. The change in mean wait can grow without bound with (H).
Even if (H) is held at a fixed permitted service limit, the wait difference remains
positive as (epsilon) tends to zero, so no Lipschitz bound proportional to the arrival
perturbation is recovered merely by imposing a service cap.

Consequently, the FCFS transport bound strengthens the interpretation of a
policy-trace shadow-FCFS reference. It does not repair the claim that an open-loop
replay of the predicted-size policy is a lower bound on its closed-loop benefit.

## Service-cost perturbations accumulate

If the service vectors also differ, write

\[
  d_i=|c_i-c'_i|.
\]

Let (D_i=|v_i-v'_i|_\infty). The same recursion gives

\[
  D_i\leq \max\{D_{i-1},\epsilon\}+d_i,
\]

and hence

\[
  |s_i-s'_i|
  \leq \epsilon+\sum_{j<i}d_j,
  \qquad
  |w_i-w'_i|
  \leq 2\epsilon+\sum_{j<i}d_j.
\]

If every service perturbation is at most (delta), the universal bound becomes
(2\epsilon+(i-1)\delta) for job (i), and
(2\epsilon+(n-1)\delta) for an arbitrary common-cohort mean or quantile. This
linear accumulation is unavoidable without more structure. For (k>1), occupy
(k-1) servers with common very long jobs and send a rank-ordered sequence of short
jobs through the remaining server. Increasing each short service by (delta)
accumulates one additional (delta) at every predecessor. Thus a small per-job
cost drift cannot be replaced by a job-count-independent additive constant.

## Arrival ranks and ties are substantive assumptions

The fixed-rank premise is stronger than a small timestamp perturbation. If two jobs
exchange FCFS order, arbitrarily small arrival changes can cause arbitrarily large
wait changes. With one effective server, let a size-(H) job and a unit job have
arrival times (0) and (eta), then exchange those two times in the second trace.
The sup-norm change is only (eta), but the unit job's wait changes from nearly
(H) to zero. The construction again extends to (k>1) by occupying the other
servers. Replicating the short job shows the same discontinuity for a tail quantile,
not only for one labelled wait.

Exact simultaneous arrivals do not invalidate the proof if both systems retain the
same externally specified tie order. They do invalidate the premise if the two runs
resolve ties differently. Completion-driven submissions can also cross submissions
from other users, so a fixed eventual set of identities does not imply a fixed
arrival rank.

## How the condition could be checked

A future controlled closed-loop comparison could use this result as an audit rather
than assume its premises:

1. Pre-specify a common finite set of job identities and one FCFS tie key.
2. Record identity-matched arrivals and executed service costs under both policies.
3. Verify that the two arrival sequences have the same total rank, then report the
   observed (epsilon=max_i|a_i^P-a_i^F|). A rank failure makes the bound
   inapplicable rather than merely wider.
4. Verify cost equality. If costs differ, report the cumulative cost-perturbation
   term instead of using (2\epsilon).
5. Replay FCFS on both realised vectors and check the per-job recursion before
   reporting the interval (B_{\rm shadow}\pm2\epsilon).

The present trace supplies only one fixed arrival vector and no independently
generated closed-loop counterfactual, so it cannot perform steps 2--4 or estimate an
empirical (epsilon). A plot over explicitly hypothetical values of (epsilon)
would be a transparent sensitivity analysis, but it would not identify feedback.

An exact defensible statement is:

> Conditional on common job identities, unchanged service requirements, a common FCFS rank including tie-breaking, and a maximum identity-wise arrival shift of \(\epsilon\), replacing policy-trace shadow FCFS by independently generated FCFS changes each job's wait and any common-cohort mean or linearly interpolated p99 by at most \(2\epsilon\). These premises and \(\epsilon\) are not identified by the current logs.
