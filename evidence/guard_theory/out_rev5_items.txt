========================================================================

The referee's counterexample, reproduced with sim_core:
  k = 1, constant budget B = 2, no global size bound.
  rank 0 = victim i (a=0, C=1); rank 1 = A (a=0, C=1); rank 2 = Z (a=1, C=M).
  base policy: the static priority 'A, then Z, then i'.

  M   W_FCFS  W_P   In_i  excess  Lam(a_i)  Lam(s^P_i)   B+k*Lam(a_i)  B+k*Lam(s^P)  add(a_i)  add(s^P)
  1    0       2     2     2       1         1           3             3             3         3        
  2    0       3     3     3       1         2           3             4             3         4        
  3    0       4     4     4       1         3           3             5             3         5

  100  0       101   101   101     1         100         3             102           3         102      
  1000 0       1001  1001  1001    1         1000        3             1002          3         1002     

  rows violating the Lambda(a_i) form of (guardin)/(guardadd): M = [2, 3, 5, 10, 100, 1000]
  rows violating the Lambda(s^P_i) form:                       M = [] (empty = the corrected placement holds)

  NOTE the referee writes 'violated for every M > 2'; the first violation is
  at M = 2, where In_i = 3 is not < 3 and excess = 3 is not < 3.

Brute force of the corrected statement, random instances, sizes UNBOUNDED
(Pareto-like integer sizes, no cap; k = 1..4; constant budget):

   k   instances  job-checks   fail Lam(a_i)[In]  fail Lam(a_i)[add]  fail Lam(s^P)[In]  fail Lam(s^P)[add]
   1   400        925          126                126                 0                  0                 
   2   400        555          23                 0                   0                  0                 
   3   400        307          7                  0                   0                  0                 
   4   400        287          0                  0                   0                  0                 

  total job-checks: 2074
  Lambda(a_i) fails in both wrapper bounds; Lambda(s^P_i) never fails.
  The sharp form proved below is
      In_i      <  Bmax + k*Lambda(s^P_i)
      excess_i  <  Bmax/k + Lambda(s^P_i) + (2-2/k)*Lambda(a_i)
  and since Lambda(a_i) <= Lambda(s^P_i) it implies the uniform statement
  with Lambda(s^P_i) in both terms.

RR-2  Impossibility is a statement about wrappers; the exact hypothesis
========================================================================

(a) FCFS is a counterexample to the proposition as printed (universally
    quantified over non-preemptive work-conserving policies that read a
    service time only at completion):
    k=1  max excess under FCFS = 0
    k=2  max excess under FCFS = 0
    k=3  max excess under FCFS = 0
    FCFS is non-preemptive, work-conserving, reads no service time, and has
    excess == 0 on every input.  So does every rank-ordered policy.

(b) Instance I1: victim + k jobs, all of service L, all at t = 0.
    Any wrapper whose budget is POSITIVE at the first dispatch epoch is
    forced to excess >= L; a wrapper whose budget is 0 there dispatches the
    victim at once.

     k   L   budget(i,0)   excess of the victim
     1   10  0             0
     1   10  1             10
     1   10  5             10
     2   10  0             0
     2   10  1             10
     2   10  5             10
     3   10  0             0
     3   10  1             10
     3   10  5             10
     4   10  0             0
     4   10  1             10
     4   10  5             10

(c) 'zero at the first epoch but positive later' does NOT escape.
    Instance I2, for the age-relative shape budget = min(B0 + eta*k*(t-a_q), Bmax)
    with B0 = 0 and eta > 0:
      t=0   : k jobs of service L (ranks 0..k-1) -- the wrapper is FCFS here,
              every budget being 0 at age 0;
      t=1   : the victim i (rank k), which waits behind them, exactly as it
              would under FCFS;
      t=L-1 : k jobs of service L (ranks k+1..2k), the overtakers.
    At t = L the victim has age L-1 > 0, so its budget is positive, the guard
    does not fire, and the base fills every server with the overtakers.

     k   L   eta      W_FCFS[i]  W_P[i]   excess   >= L ?
     1   10  1/2      9          19       10       True
     1   10  1/10     9          19       10       True
     2   10  1/2      9          19       10       True
     2   10  1/10     9          19       10       True
     3   10  1/2      9          19       10       True
     3   10  1/10     9          19       10       True

(d) The only budget rules that escape BOTH instances are the degenerate ones,
    and they reproduce first-come first-served exactly.  If budget(q,t) = 0
    whenever over[q](t) = 0, then at every dispatch epoch the lowest-ranked
    waiting job h has over[h] = 0 (by induction: nothing has ever overtaken
    anything), hence h is in E(t) and is its minimum-rank member, hence h is
    dispatched.  Checked on random instances against the FCFS order:
    600 instances, 0 disagreements with the FCFS dispatch order.

RR-8  over[q](t) <= In^exec_q <= In_q : completed work bounds both

RR-26  the implied constant in epsilon = O((m+n)/(mn))
========================================================================

With  N = (1/k) m (L-s)(kG + (3k-2)L)/s,  D = (1/k) m n (L-s),
      E = 2(1-1/k) L (m+n),  the proof gives  ratio <= (N+E)/(D-E),  so
      epsilon = (N+E)/(D-E) - N/D = E (N+D) / (D (D-E)),
      epsilon ~ (E/D)(1 + N/D) = [2(k-1)L(m+n)/(m n (L-s))] * (1 + N/D).
So the implied constant is
      C = 2(k-1) L (1 + (kG+(3k-2)L)/(n s)) / (L - s),
NOT the referee's 2(1-1/k) L (1 + ...)/(L-s): E/D carries a factor k, because
D itself has the 1/k.  (The two agree at k = 1, where both vanish.)
