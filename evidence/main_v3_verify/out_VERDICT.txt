C5  FINITE-SKIP BASELINE -- PARTIALLY CONFIRMED (F4, NOTE)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The baseline is given N = G*k/L - (3k-2) skips (30 at k = 4, G = 600; 9 at k = 1), which follows
from the kernel's accounting: the count channel charges a skip only when the overtaker COMPLETES
(guardkern.py docstring, "COUNT CHANNEL"), so up to k-1 overtakers in service plus the one being
dispatched are uncounted and the conversion needs the extra k*L.  Charging the skip at DISPATCH
time instead -- the usual reading of "a job may be passed at most N times", and what a position
counter in the prior art does -- bounds the inserted work by N*L directly and gives
        W <= W_FCFS + (N + 2k - 2) L / k,
so the same promise G allows N = G*k/L - (2k-2): 34 instead of 30 at k = 4 / G = 600, and 10
instead of 9 at k = 1, i.e. 11-13 % more skips.  The baseline is therefore mildly handicapped.
Measured (my simulator, the looser count, out_cell_primary_rep0_L2.txt and the k = 1 log):
        primary rep0 level 2   SKIP-G600 p99_dl 262.513 -> 262.545 with N = 34 (max excess 100.6 s)
        k = 1                  SKIP-G600 p99_dl 219.953 -> 219.885 with N = 10 (max excess 100.9 s)
The gap closed stays at about 0.002 (k = 4) and 0.008 (k = 1).  The conclusion "position counting
buys essentially nothing on this load" survives the fairer configuration; the report's own
sentence about it should nevertheless be stated with the dispatch-time count, or the smaller N
should be justified in the text.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * The per-job guarantee is empirical fact on this data under an independent implementation:
    5.7e9 of my own per-job checks (16 reported cells plus a 144-run validation grid), 0
    violations, including the three adversarial predictors and k = 1.
  * The scheduling numbers of sections 3-7 and 9 are reproducible: an independently written
    simulator fed the same traces reproduces every one of them (1,016 of 1,364 checked directly
    against my own runs, the rest against the stored cells).
  * At the busiest hour rho ~ 1.0 with k = 4, ranking by the Tweedie expected cost closes
    0.916 [0.892, 0.929] of the FCFS -> true-size-SJF gap in the deadline window, and beats the

  * The guard converts an unbounded tail into the promised one: worst excess over FCFS 5,905 s

    (F7).  The 1.2-4.7x claim is for G = 300, G = 1200 and k = 1.

    same rule rejects them as infeasible and picks (120*k/4, 0.75) instead (F8).  The G = 300 and
    G = 1200 settings are stable across both validation overlays and may be described as chosen
    on validation data.

    construction, five overlays that are not five platforms.
  * Do not claim R1S-Tweedie improves on Tweedie (unresolved at rho 1.0, worse at rho 0.5), and do
    not claim an end-to-end result: feature and prediction cost is not charged in these runs.
