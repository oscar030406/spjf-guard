\section{Cost Prediction}

\subsection{The Visibility Audit in Full}

Section~4.2 of the manuscript states that every historical feature is recomputed by
streaming the log in availability order and reading each entity's state as of the query
instant, and that \devnum{0} rows disagree in any of the eight configurations. This is
what that test is.

On \devnum{3{,}000} randomly drawn development jobs all \devnum{34} history and
relational features are recomputed from scratch under each configuration's own
visibility rule and compared against the pipeline's values. The same test applied to the
natural but wrong construction, in which a feature may use any record with a smaller
index, fails on \devnum{97.67\%} of rows under the primary rule, so the test does
discriminate. The graph pipeline is checked separately: an index assertion over
\devnum{863{,}149} rows finds \devnum{0} timeline entries pointing at or after the read,
brute-force recomputation on \devnum{600} rows matches on all \devnum{2{,}400} root
states and all \devnum{2{,}400} neighbour sets, and perturbing or deleting every record
after a cut time leaves all earlier predictions unchanged while a positive control moves
the later ones.

\subsection{The Release Delay as a Sensitivity}
\label{sec:supp_lag}

The delay $\delta$ with which results reach the feature store is treated as a
sensitivity and not as an assumption, which is the third of the three commitments
Section~4.2 of the manuscript makes. At $\delta = 0$ the full tabular model reaches
AUROC \devnum{0.9283} and at $\delta = \devnum{600}$ s it reaches \devnum{0.9066}. On the second judging
platform, where the log carries no clock and lag has to be expressed in records, the same
model moves from \devnum{0.9266} at lag \devnum{0} to \devnum{0.8902} at lag
\devnum{1{,}000}. Ranking survives a ten-minute feedback delay at the top of the
curve; retrieval of the small heavy class degrades, average precision falling from
\devnum{0.289} to \devnum{0.139}.

\subsection{Predictor Families}
\label{sec:supp_families}

Three model families are compared under one visibility protocol and one split: gradient-boosted
trees, a sequence model, and a relational ladder. The negative relational result of
Section~4.3 of the manuscript is read off the last of them.


The tabular family is gradient-boosted trees~\cite{ke2017lightgbm} over five feature groups:
task history (M1), user history (M2), static features of the submitted artefact (M3),
everything tabular (M4), and M4 plus one-hop relational aggregates over the
task-assignment and user-cohort neighbourhoods (M5). A control (M6) permutes task
identity across assignments, so that M5's aggregates are keyed on a scrambled
neighbourhood; it reaches AUROC \devnum{0.9288} in the primary configuration against
M5's \devnum{0.9302}, so the aggregates lose almost nothing when the identity they are
built on is destroyed. The sequence family is a GRU over the earlier jobs of one
user whose results are already available, used alone (R1) and as a state vector appended
to M4's columns inside the boosted model (R1S). The relational family is a heterogeneous
network over user, task, assignment and cohort nodes~\cite{schlichtkrull2018rgcn} with no
node-identity embeddings, so an unseen task or user is handled by the same weights. Its
ablation ladder runs N0 (a multilayer perceptron on exactly M4's columns), G0U (the
relational architecture with every neighbour slot empty and the assignment and cohort
roots removed, which leaves exactly M4's information), G0 (empty neighbour slots, roots
kept), G1 (the real edges) and G2 (the same network on edges permuted within a period and
time block, so that neighbour counts and recency are preserved and only the identity of
the neighbour changes).

\subsection{The Cost of Predicting}
\label{sec:supp_predcost}

Prediction has to be cheap enough to sit in front of a queue whose median job is
\devnum{204} ms, which is what makes the choice between these predictors a queueing
decision and not a latency one. Measured in a streaming loop over \devnum{20{,}000} jobs, the
tabular path costs a median of \devnum{0.076} ms per job and a 99th percentile of
\devnum{0.188} ms; the relational network costs \devnum{0.434} ms per row plus
\devnum{0.121} ms of graph lookup, and the GRU \devnum{0.576} ms. Charging the measured
feature and inference latency to the predictive policies before they enqueue changes the
closed gap by less than \devnum{0.001} at every load we ran, so the choice between these
predictors is not a latency decision at this job size.

\section{Traces and Workload Characterisation}
\label{sec:supp_data}

\subsection{The Field Audit of the Main Judging Trace}

Section~7.2 of the manuscript states four readings of the main trace. This is the
evidence for them, and the two archive defects that follow from reading the trace this
way.

\paragraph{The timestamp is a result stamp.}
Among later-period runs the gap to the same user's previous event grows one for one with
the cost (slope \devnum{0.99} of the median gap on $C$) while the gap to the next event
barely moves (slope \devnum{0.16}), and the feedback after a user kills a run arrives
within about \devnum{0.1} s whatever its length. A result stamp predicts both readings; a
receipt stamp neither.

\paragraph{The whole-second leak.}
Stamps are recorded to the whole second while costs are recorded to the microsecond, so
the fractional part of a job's cost appears in the gap between its own stamp and the next
event, and a feature like ``seconds since the user's previous record'' reads the label off
the clock. We close it by replacing $t$ with $t + u$, where $u \in [0,1)$ is drawn
deterministically from a fixed key and the record's identifier.

\paragraph{The cost field and the limit.}
The cost field is wall-clock occupancy of one evaluation including interpreter start-up,
read from the 10th percentile of correct submissions (\devnum{0.12}--\devnum{0.18} s
across the two periods) and from its weak dependence on the number of test cases (median
\devnum{$0.196 + 0.007 n$} s in the later period). The platform limit is \devnum{60} s:
the last run to reach it ends at \devnum{2019-08-28} \devnum{10{:}32}, after which the
largest observed cost is \devnum{59.97} s, with \devnum{13} runs in $[59,60)$ s and none
above. Before that date there was no fixed limit, the longest run is \devnum{8{,}901} s,
and the \devnum{1{,}189} runs that end with a kill are almost all above \devnum{360.8} s,
pointing at a container watchdog rather than judging demand.

\paragraph{Co-ending groups, zero-cost blocks and two short archives.}
Groups of runs by the same user that end in the same second are \devnum{7.53\%} of blocks
and \devnum{26.28\%} of capped work; every prediction table in the manuscript is repeated
on rows outside such groups. A further \devnum{1{,}671} blocks record a cost of exactly
zero with empty outputs; they leave the simulated trace and stay in the features, the
labels and every total. The \devnum{2019-2} archive stops at \devnum{2019-09-19}, months
before that term's assessments end, and \devnum{2020-1} is short by about \devnum{3\%} of
its blocks; both serve per-submission modelling and are excluded from anything that treats
a term as a whole.

\paragraph{Against the published release statistics.}
We match the class count in \devnum{18} of \devnum{18} terms and the student and exercise
counts in \devnum{16} of \devnum{18}, and parse \devnum{4.08\%} fewer execution blocks
than the official column sums, whose printed totals disagree with those same columns by
\devnum{$-248$} exercises and \devnum{$+246{,}246$} blocks. We quote the columns.

\subsection{The Continuous-Integration Refit: Sharpness and Circularity}

Section~7.3 of the manuscript reports the held-out refit of the effective capacity
$k_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and the setup term on the two Firefox CI pools. Two checks on that fit
sit here.

The objective is sharp in $k$ rather than flat: on a step-of-one grid only \devnum{5} to
\devnum{8} values of $k$ put the simulated mean wait within \devnum{10\%} of the recorded
one, so the capacity the fit chooses is not one of many equally good answers.

The circularity of the original whole-window fit can be read off the two pools directly.
On pool~A the whole-window and held-out rows are almost identical, because every split
picks nearly the same capacity; on pool~B the reverse split's held-out capacity gives a
mean ratio of \devnum{1.14} on its test part where the whole-window capacity gives
\devnum{0.99}. The difference between those two numbers is the size of the circularity
that fitting on the reported window would have hidden.

What the split does not cover is stated in Section~9 of the manuscript and belongs here
in full. Out of sample the 90th percentile lands up to \devnum{21\%} from the recorded
one and is systematically low on the larger pool. The split is chronological inside one
\devnum{21}-day and one \devnum{7}-day window of two pools, not a different cluster,
quarter or workload, and the capacity is held constant over the test part although the
real machine count varies.

\subsection{What Supports the Shared-Pool Design}
\label{sec:supp_pool}

Two of the three things Section~7.3 of the manuscript names in support of the
shared-pool design are measurements rather than arguments, and both are made here.

The second is a measurement on the same demand. Replaying the overlay of
Section~7.4 of the manuscript under the dedicated design, the 99th percentile of containers
alive at once is \devnum{1{,}636} and each container executes code for \devnum{0.12\%} of
the time it is alive; since only \devnum{11\%} to \devnum{35\%} of logins have a logout
written after them, session boundaries are reconstructed, and across six reconstructions
that percentile lies between \devnum{1{,}003} and \devnum{2{,}525} containers and the
utilisation between \devnum{0.07\%} and \devnum{0.17\%}. A shared pool holds the 99th
percentile of deadline-window waits under \devnum{1} s with \devnum{14} servers under
FCFS and \devnum{13} under the predictor-ordered policy. Pooling is worth two orders of
magnitude here and the scheduler is worth one server (\devnum{14} to \devnum{13} at
\devnum{1} s, \devnum{11} to \devnum{10} at \devnum{5} s, \devnum{8} to \devnum{7} at
\devnum{30} s). It does not support a claim that the platform ever queued, the pooled
side being simulated, nor that the containers are over-provisioned, since they also run
the editing session the pool does not replace, nor that any of it is a cost saving.

The third is that the simulator reproduces waits somebody else recorded. The CI logs carry
the queue wait of every run, so we replay the recorded arrivals and services through our
own $k$-server non-preemptive kernel. Two quantities are fitted: the effective capacity
$k_{\mathrm{eff}}$, the shortfall against the listed machine count being machines that are
up but not claiming work, and a setup term, the median short gap between consecutive runs
on the same machine, added to every service because a machine is not reusable the instant
a run resolves. Fitting both on the window and then reporting agreement on that same
window would be circular, so we refit both on one chronological part of each window and
test on the other, five splits over the two pools.
This split is internal to the recorded window and is held out of the fit only; it
is not sealed in the sense of Section~7.6 of the manuscript, and the whole window has
been read.

The capacity is stable across splits. On pool~A the three splits choose \devnum{155},
\devnum{155} and \devnum{157} of \devnum{173} listed machines, and on pool~B the two
splits choose \devnum{96} and \devnum{92} of \devnum{105}; the setup term moves between
\devnum{54.2} and \devnum{55.3} s on the first and between \devnum{126.2} and
\devnum{128.6} s on the second. The objective is sharp in $k$. With those parameters
frozen, the held-out part comes
back at \devnum{0.90} to \devnum{1.14} times the recorded mean wait, \devnum{0.86} to
\devnum{1.16} at the 99th percentile and \devnum{0.89} to \devnum{1.28} at the 99.9th,
with a per-hour mean-wait correlation of \devnum{0.936} to \devnum{0.981}, a per-job rank
correlation of \devnum{0.940} to \devnum{0.963}, and a median per-job absolute error of
\devnum{16} to \devnum{84} s.

The 90th percentile does not survive the held-out test. It lands at \devnum{0.81} to
\devnum{1.21}
of the recorded one, and on the larger pool it is below the recorded value on all three
splits while the 99th is above it, so the simulated distribution is too steep in its upper
middle; the two-term objective hides this in sample by trading the mean against the p90,
so we fit out of sample and say so. What the fit absorbs is nonetheless a
property of the pool and no artefact of fitting: told instead that every listed
machine is available, the same simulator underestimates the held-out mean wait by a factor
of \devnum{1.3} to \devnum{2.6} and its per-hour correlation falls to \devnum{0.75}.
Section~S2.2 gives the step-of-one sharpness check and reads the circularity
of the original whole-window fit off the two pools directly.

\paragraph{Arrival and service provenance across traces.}
CodeBench's jittered result stamp minus the recorded duration is an execution-start
proxy, not a measured receipt time. ACcoding has no submission clock: arrivals are
drawn, including the Poisson comparison and the deadline-shaped construction; its
submission ids determine ordering, not elapsed time. Its measured cost sums test
case execution times, each subject to its problem limit, and the replay adds an
assumed \devnum{0.5} s overhead. Thus neither the observed \devnum{30.0} s sum nor
the resulting \devnum{30.5} s maximum is a verified whole-job timeout.
% Source: evidence/accoding_v2/out_accoding_v2.txt, field audit and compare section B.

For Azure, the replay arrival is the documented end timestamp minus duration,
again a start proxy; the publisher explicitly states that invocation timestamps
were modified from the production trace \cite{azure2021trace}. The Netbatch replay
uses the recorded SWF submission time and run duration. Their bounds in
Table~\ref{tab:s_tails} are training p99.9 truncations, not observed platform timeouts.
Both FCFS and every reordered policy receive the same truncated service sequence.
The truncation affects \devnum{1{,}187} Azure jobs and \devnum{7{,}696} Netbatch jobs
in the analysed pools; it removes \devnum{3.18\%} and \devnum{2.16\%} of raw work.
% Sources: evidence/cross_domain/meta_azure.csv and meta_netbatch.csv;
% evidence/cross_domain/out_leaktest_azure.txt and out_leaktest_netbatch.txt,
% clipped counts; out_audit_azure.txt and out_audit_netbatch.txt, work removed.

In the CI pools, arrival is the recorded scheduled instant, when dependencies have
cleared and a worker may claim the run; recorded wait is started minus scheduled.
Service is resolved minus started plus a fitted same-worker setup term. The analysis
first adds setup and then clips this occupancy at the training p99.9 $L$;
longer jobs are retained with truncated service, not excluded. This clips
\devnum{227} runs in pool~A and \devnum{27} in pool~B. FCFS and all comparison
policies use exactly that same capped occupancy sequence. The source scheduler's
priority-then-FIFO rule and operational capacity are distinct from the counterfactual
FCFS model used for the guarantee.
% Sources: evidence/firefox_ci/out_leaktest_releng-hardware__gecko-t-osx-1500-m4.txt and
% out_leaktest_releng-hardware__gecko-t-linux-talos-2404.txt;
% evidence/firefox_ci/meta_releng-hardware__gecko-t-osx-1500-m4.csv and
% meta_releng-hardware__gecko-t-linux-talos-2404.csv; fci_build.py and fci_sim.py.

An enforced $L$ in a deployed service would instead kill an unfinished job at the
limit and charge that executed work to the same-input FCFS comparison. The killed
job would not yield its intended result; these replays do not estimate the resulting
completion-quality cost or users' resubmission response. The table therefore
supports finite-input bounds under its stated transformations, not a future
operational timeout claim.

\subsection{Outcome Visibility: Exposure, Lag and the Variants}
\label{sec:supp_visibility}

Three things stand behind the conservative-visibility headline of Section~8.3 of the
manuscript: the same-copy queueing exposure it conditions on, an audit of the release
lag, and the two controls with the pending exact variant that its table names. All three
are defined here.

Let $H_i$ be the set of distinct outcome records used by score $i$, counting a record
only once if it enters several entity aggregates. Separate records from the same
class-term copy, histories from other classes or terms, and records with no
submission job in the replayed pool. Same-copy queueing exposure is
\[
 V_i(P)=\{j\in H_i:\ \operatorname{copy}(j)=\operatorname{copy}(i),\quad
             a_j+W_P[j]+C_j>a_i\}.
\]
An external past record is not unavailable merely because it has no replayed job.
The earlier audit mapped histories through the same replication round while
different classes were independently shifted. Its roughly \devnum{80\%} affected
jobs and \devnum{95.39\%} with unreplayed history are mapping-exposure statistics,
not estimates of same-copy queueing leakage. Section~S5 specifies
the external-history clock and the separate class-history sensitivity.
% Source: outputs/dev_visibility/visibility_exposure.csv, overall_affected_share
% (approximately 0.80 across cells), unreplayed_overall_affected_share=0.9538935602.

The conservative score restricts user, exercise and pair history to submission
outcomes in the same class-term copy, and admits a result only when
$\mathrm{done}_j+\devnum{3600}\le a_i$, with times in seconds. The static score
uses code, course and clock information known at submission, without outcome
history. Both use their own fitted models; original, conservative and static
therefore differ in more than release delay. Their differences cannot be attributed
to queueing delay alone and are not mathematical upper or lower performance bounds.
Guard parameters remain those selected under the original score. Consistent with
the committed visibility decision, the conservative variant is the empirical
headline; the original-score results are optimistic references.
% Source: outputs/dev_visibility/manifest.json, notes.conservative_lag_s,
% headline_variant and selected_parameters_unchanged.

For a same-copy submission, $W_P[j]\le D$ is a sufficient availability condition
under a source release lag $D$. The largest development FCFS wait is
\devnum{1544.602708} s. Adding the largest evaluated promise, \devnum{1200} s,
still lies below the \devnum{3600} s lag. All conservative Guard and FCFS cells
have \devnum{0} lag violations. Unguarded conservative SPJF-E has
\devnum{336} job occurrences with waits beyond the lag across the evaluated cells;
it lacks the guard's certificate, so its performance is a lagged-score comparator
with residual availability violations. A new pool must pass the per-job lag audit;
the development maximum does not certify it in advance.
% Source: outputs/dev_visibility/visibility_waits_and_lag.csv, max(wait_max_s)
% for FCFS; sum(lag_violations) for conservative SPJF-E=336 and for FCFS/Guard=0;
% outputs/dev_visibility/manifest.json, promises and conservative_lag_s.

The exact variant freezes the original model weights, replays each policy, withholds
same-copy outcomes still unavailable at the target arrival, recomputes affected
histories and scores, and repeats until no used outcome violates availability.
Withheld sets only grow, so this finite procedure terminates. It certifies
availability in its final replay; it is not a claim of minimal withholding, a unique
fixed point, or an online predictor driven by completion events. A full comparison
and the associated exposure decomposition remain pending. The conservative and static
results above are the completed controls; no partial exact result is used to select
parameters or replace the headline.

\subsection{What the Shape of the Budget Is Worth}
\label{sec:supp_shape}

The two shapes the main table no longer prints are read here through the paired differences and
in full in Table~\ref{tab:s_guard_abl}, all three at one fixed promise. The
shape matters where the promise is tight and the load is high, and hardly at all elsewhere. At
$G = \devnum{600}$ s the constant shape trails the joint winner by
\devnum{0.007 [0.003, 0.012]}, \devnum{0.028 [0.020, 0.038]} and \devnum{0.119 [0.074, 0.176]}
of the gap as the load rises through the three levels, each interval clear of zero; at
$G = \devnum{300}$ s the same three differences are \devnum{0.008 [-0.002, 0.015]},
\devnum{0.099 [0.056, 0.157]} and \devnum{0.262 [0.202, 0.288]}; and at $G = \devnum{1200}$ s
they are \devnum{$-0.001$}, \devnum{$-0.001$} and \devnum{0.003}, none of the three intervals
excluding zero. At a loose promise and a light load the shape is not doing measurable work.

The other axis is harm. At $G = \devnum{600}$ s the queue-length shape reaches within
\devnum{0.009} of the joint winner's gap at every load while its worst-overlay harm is
\devnum{106.0}, \devnum{117.7} and \devnum{123.4} s against the winner's \devnum{237.0},
\devnum{319.6} and \devnum{339.9} s, under half at every load. That is also why
the rule picks the queue-length shape at $G = \devnum{1200}$ s, where the three shapes are
within \devnum{0.005} of one another on the validation objective and it has the smallest worst
harm. The shape decides which jobs pay for the promise more than it decides how much of the
benefit survives.

Fixed($G$), which spends the whole cap, is an equal-promise candidate in the search,
but is infeasible under the validation harm constraint at every promise.
Its evaluation-overlay harm is \devnum{181.8}, \devnum{195.4} and \devnum{208.2} s at
$G = \devnum{300}$ s against a \devnum{150} s limit, \devnum{237.0}, \devnum{447.1} and
\devnum{465.5} s at $G = \devnum{600}$ s against \devnum{300} s, and \devnum{237.0},
\devnum{469.2} and \devnum{708.8} s at $G = \devnum{1200}$ s against \devnum{600} s.
Thus the evaluation cells also exceed the corresponding limits at each promise.
It is ahead of the joint winner on the gap
closed in eight of the nine cells, by up to \devnum{0.042}, so it buys its extra gap with harm
above the declared limit in some cells; we make no claim that any shape beats it on
the gap at equal promise.

\section{Deferred Proofs}
\label{sec:supp_proofs}

Section~6 of the manuscript gives the ideas behind the arguments proved here. The
workload comparison uses continuity and left limits, whereas consistency is proved by
induction; the strictness at $k \ge 2$ runs the same way as the bound itself, in that at
the first instant $t_1$ at which $D = U_{Q_1} - U_{Q_2}$ touches $(k-1)L$ the linear
piece to its left must have positive slope, which forces at most $k-1$ jobs present
under $Q_1$ there, so none of them waits, and left limits then make $U_{Q_1}$ strictly
decreasing on that piece, so it cannot equal its own supremum at $t_1$. The consistency
induction needs two further readings, both one line: its first consequence uses
$\mathrm{over}[q](t) \le \mathrm{In}^A_q$, a completed overtaker of $q$ having been
dispatched while $q$ waited, and the second uses Corollary~2 of the manuscript. The
tightness constructions and the remaining proofs are
Section~\ref{sec:supp_constructions} of this file. We use the manuscript's notation:
$U_Q(t)$ is the unfinished work at time $t$ under policy $Q$, and $N_Q(t)$ is the
number of jobs present.

\subsection{The Workload Comparison}
\label{sec:supp_gap}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~1 of the manuscript]
Put $D = U_{Q_1} - U_{Q_2}$. Arrivals add the same jump to both terms, so $D$ is
continuous, and between arrivals it changes at rate
$-(\min(N_{Q_1},k) - \min(N_{Q_2},k))$; it is therefore piecewise linear with
breakpoints among the finitely many arrival and completion instants of the two
runs.

If $U_{Q_1}(u) > (k-1)L$ then more than $k-1$ jobs are present under $Q_1$, each
carrying at most $L$, so every server of $Q_1$ is busy and
$\min(N_{Q_1},k) = k \ge \min(N_{Q_2},k)$: $D$ does not increase at $u$. Fix $t$
and let
$S = \{u \le t : U_{Q_1}(u) \le (k-1)L\}$, which is non-empty because $U_{Q_1}$
vanishes
before the first arrival, and put $t_0 = \sup S$. The supremum need not be
attained, since $U_{Q_1}$ jumps upwards at arrivals, so we use the left limit:
there are $u_n \uparrow t_0$ with $U_{Q_1}(u_n) \le (k-1)L$, hence
$U_{Q_1}(t_0^-) \le (k-1)L$ and, $D$ being continuous,
$D(t_0^-) \le U_{Q_1}(t_0^-) \le (k-1)L$ because $U_{Q_2} \ge 0$. On $(t_0, t]$
we have
$U_{Q_1} > (k-1)L$, so $D$ does not increase there, and $D(t) \le (k-1)L$.
Exchanging $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ gives the other direction. That the constant cannot be
lowered is the cascade of Section~\ref{sec:supp_cascade} of this file, which reaches
$(k-1)L\,(1 - ((k-1)/k)^m)$ after $m$ rounds. At $k = 1$ the bound reads
$|D| \le 0$ and is attained everywhere: one server executes work at rate $1$
whenever any job is present, so $U_{Q_1} \equiv U_{Q_2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the strict inequality in Lemma~1, for $k \ge 2$]
Keep the notation above, so $D = U_{Q_1} - U_{Q_2}$ is continuous and piecewise
linear with finitely many pieces. Suppose $D(t) = (k-1)L$ for some $t$ and let
$t_1$ be the smallest such $t$; it exists because $\{D = (k-1)L\}$ is closed and
$D(0) = 0 < (k-1)L$, which uses $L > 0$ and $k \ge 2$.

Let $I$ be the linear piece immediately to the left of $t_1$, which has positive
length. Its slope is strictly positive: a negative slope would put
$D > (k-1)L$ somewhere on $I$ and a zero slope would put $D = (k-1)L$ on all of
$I$, and either contradicts the minimality of $t_1$. The slope of $D$ is
$-(\min(N_{Q_1},k) - \min(N_{Q_2},k))$, so $\min(N_{Q_1},k) < \min(N_{Q_2},k)$
on $I$ and
therefore $N_{Q_1} \le k-1$ there.

With at most $k-1$ jobs present and $Q_1$ work-conserving, none of $Q_1$'s jobs
waits, so all of them are in service; hence $U_{Q_1}$ falls at rate $N_{Q_1}$ on
$I$,
and $U_{Q_1} \le (k-1)L$ on $I$ because each of the at most $k-1$ present jobs
carries at most $L$. Taking left limits at $t_1$ and using the continuity of
$D$,
\[
  U_{Q_1}(t_1^-) - U_{Q_2}(t_1^-) = (k-1)L , \qquad U_{Q_1}(t_1^-) \le (k-1)L ,
  \qquad U_{Q_2} \ge 0 ,
\]
which forces $U_{Q_2}(t_1^-) = 0$ and $U_{Q_1}(t_1^-) = (k-1)L$. Since $L > 0$
and $k \ge 2$, this
gives $N_{Q_1} \ge 1$ on $I$---with $N_{Q_1} = 0$ we would have
$U_{Q_1}(t_1^-) = 0 = (k-1)L$---so $U_{Q_1}$ is strictly decreasing on $I$ and
$U_{Q_1}(t_1^-) < \sup_I U_{Q_1} \le (k-1)L$, a contradiction. Exchanging $Q_1$
and $Q_2$ gives the other direction. Both steps that invoke $(k-1)L > 0$ fail at
$k = 1$, where the statement is false: there $U_{Q_1} \equiv U_{Q_2}$ and the
bound $0$ is attained at every instant.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Consistency, and Why the Converse Fails}
\label{sec:supp_consistency}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~1 of the manuscript]
Induct on dispatch epochs: up to the first epoch at which the two runs could
differ their completion histories agree, hence so does $\mathrm{over}[\cdot]$,
so $E(t)$ is empty in the wrapped run and it copies $A$. The first consequence
uses $\mathrm{over}[q](t) \le \mathrm{In}^A_q$, a completed overtaker of $q$
having been dispatched while $q$ waited; the second uses Corollary~2 of the
manuscript.
\end{proof}

The converse fails, so this is not an equivalence. Take $k = 1$, two jobs of
service $1$ at $t = 0$, the base policy ``higher rank first'', and $B = 1$. Both
runs dispatch in the order $(1,0)$, yet at the second dispatch the waiting job
has $\mathrm{over} = 1 \ge B$, so the guard fires and happens to choose what the
base would have chosen. Nor is there multiplicative consistency: with $k = 1$,
one job of service $L$ followed by $m$ jobs of service $1$ all at $t = 0$, the
wrapper with $B = 0$ is first-come first-served while the base ordering by
service time is not, and the ratio of mean waits is $(2L + m - 1)/(m+1)$,
unbounded in $L$.

\subsection{The Net-Overtake Identity, and Its Two Directions}
\label{sec:supp_directions}

The net-overtake identity is proved here in full; the manuscript keeps the statement,
the busy-period accounting (1) and the idea. A remark follows on why its two extremes
do not have the same content, and on what the executed-work convention would change.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~1 of the manuscript]
\emph{Busy-period accounting.} Job $i$ waits throughout $[a_i, s^P_i)$, so by
work conservation every server is busy on that interval and exactly $k\,W_P[i]$
units of work are executed there, none of it on $i$. (If $s^P_i = a_i$ the
interval is empty and both sides vanish.) Split that work by rank.

A job $j$ of rank above $i$ has $a_j \ge a_i$, so it executes inside the window
only if it is dispatched inside it, that is only if $j \prec i$; conversely every
such $j$ is dispatched at or before $s^P_i$. The work executed on jobs of rank
above $i$ is therefore $\mathrm{In}_i - \rho^{\mathrm{new}}$, where
$\rho^{\mathrm{new}}$ is the remaining work at $s^P_i$ of those overtakers not
finished by then; a same-phase overtaker contributes $C_j$ to both terms and $0$
to the executed work, as it must.

Every job of rank below $i$ is present at $a_i$, with total remaining $R^P_i$.
At $s^P_i$ their total remaining is $\mathrm{Out}_i$, the jobs not dispatched
before $i$ each carrying their full service time, plus $\rho^{\mathrm{old}}$,
the unfinished part of those dispatched strictly before $i$, so the work
executed on them is $R^P_i - \mathrm{Out}_i - \rho^{\mathrm{old}}$. Adding the
two parts gives equation~(1) of the manuscript,
$k\,W_P[i] = R^P_i + \mathrm{In}_i - \mathrm{Out}_i - \rho^P_i$ with
$\rho^P_i := \rho^{\mathrm{old}} + \rho^{\mathrm{new}}$.

\emph{Bounding $\rho$.} The jobs counted in $\rho^P_i$ occupy distinct servers,
and none of them occupies the server that takes $i$, so there are at most $k-1$
of them and each has remaining work at most $L$: $0 \le \rho^P_i \le (k-1)L$.

\emph{The reference policy.} Under first-come first-served,
$\mathrm{In}_i = 0$, since a job of rank above $i$ dispatched before $i$ would
have to be chosen at an instant at which $i$ waits, and
$\mathrm{Out}_i = 0$, since at $s^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i$ every job of rank below $i$
has already been dispatched. So (1) reads
$k\,W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] = R^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i - \rho^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i$ with
$0 \le \rho^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i \le (k-1)L$.

\emph{Comparison.} At $a_i$, after the arrivals there are processed, the two
systems contain the same jobs, and those of rank $i$ or above are untouched in
both, so $U_P(a_i) - U_{\mathrm{FCFS}}(a_i) = R^P_i - R^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i$ and
Lemma~1 of the manuscript gives $|R^P_i - R^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i| \le (k-1)L$, strictly
for $k \ge 2$. Subtracting
the two instances of (1) gives the identity. Each direction uses
only two of the three ranges: upward
$R^P_i - R^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i \le (k-1)L$ and $\rho^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i \le (k-1)L$
with $\rho^P_i \ge 0$, and downward the mirror image, so the slack is
$2(k-1)L$ and not $3(k-1)L$. At $k = 1$ all three terms vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The upward and downward extremes have different content]
\label{rem:directions}
Upward, the extremal value is a statement about delay: on the witnesses we have
computed exactly, none of $\mathrm{In}_i$ is same-phase work and the victim's own
excess is about $+3L$. Downward, half of the extremal value is an accounting
convention. There $\mathrm{In}_i = (k-1)L$ consists entirely of overtakers
dispatched in the victim's own phase, which execute nothing while it waits; the
same-phase share of $|\Delta_i|$ is \devnum{50\%} to \devnum{63\%} at
$k = 2, 3, 4$ (Section~S8.2), and the downward victim's excess is
negative, since it starts earlier under $P$ than under first-come first-served.
If $\mathrm{In}_i$ is redefined as the work of higher-ranked jobs actually
\emph{executed} during $[a_i, s^P_i)$, Theorem~1 of the manuscript holds with the
same constant and the same proof, the upward supremum is still $2(k-1)L$, and
the best downward witness we have falls to $(k-1)L$. We keep the
dispatch-sequence convention for two reasons. Its constant is exactly determined
in both directions, whereas the downward constant for the executed-work variant
is open, somewhere in $((k-1)L, 2(k-1)L]$; and its two sides are symmetric.
Completed work lower-bounds both currencies, so the guard is implementable
either way: a job $j$ of rank above $i$ that completes while $i$ waits has
$a_j \ge a_i$ and is dispatched at or after $a_i$, so all of $C_j$ is executed
inside $[a_i, s^P_i)$ and is counted in full by the executed-work variant as
well.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What a Count of Overtakes Costs}
\label{sec:supp_counts}

A count bound on overtakes buys a per-job guarantee only through the worst-case charge
of $L$ per pass, and is not necessary for one. Both halves of Remark~1 of the
manuscript are proved below.

How the count is charged decides which guarantee. Charged when an overtaker is
\emph{dispatched}, every overtaker of $i$ goes out at an instant at which no job fires,
since a firing dispatch serves the queue head and the head is not an overtaker of $i$;
so at most $\kappa$ overtakers are dispatched while $i$ waits, which is what gives
$\mathrm{In}_i \le \kappa L$ and equation~(2) of the manuscript. Charging the count at
completion instead leaves up to $k-1$ overtakers in service and the one being dispatched
uncounted, which costs a further $L$ on the right and gives the baseline fewer passes at
the same promise.

 Both halves are below. The sufficiency uses the bound on
service times, and without that bound a single

$t = 0$ let $k-1$ jobs of service $X$, then a job of service $\epsilon$, then one
more job of service $X$ arrive together; first-come first-served starts the

Necessity fails with no such caveat: normalise to $k=1$, $L=1$ and $G=1/2$.

$1/2$, uses $\mathrm{In}_i=kG$ exactly, and lets the overtake count $M$ exceed
any fixed limit.


\subsection{Proofs of the Guard Bounds}
\label{sec:supp_guardproofs}

The additive guarantee of the wrapper holds under every budget rule that respects the
cap, and the age-relative shape carries a multiplicative form as well. Both proofs
follow.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~3 of the manuscript]
For (3) of the manuscript, let $j$ be the overtaker of $i$ dispatched last in the
dispatch sequence and let $t$ be its instant. Because dispatches are sequential
and $i$ is dispatched after $j$, job $i$ is waiting at the moment $j$ is
dispatched; this includes the case $t = s^P_i$, in which $j$ is dispatched in
$i$'s own phase, just before $i$. Since $j$ is dispatched, either $E(t)$ is empty
or its minimum-rank member is $j$; in both cases $i \notin E(t)$, because $i$ has
the smaller rank. Hence
$\mathrm{over}[i](t) < \mathrm{budget}(i,t) \le B_{\max}$.

The overtakers of $i$ dispatched strictly before $j$ split into those completed
by $t$, whose total service is $\mathrm{over}[i](t)$, and those still in service
at $t$; the latter occupy distinct servers, none of them the server taking $j$,
so there are at most $k-1$ of them, each of service at most $L$. Adding $j$
itself gives $\mathrm{In}_i < \mathrm{budget}(i,t) + kL \le B_{\max} + kL$.

For (4) of the manuscript, Theorem~1 of the manuscript with
$\mathrm{Out}_i \ge 0$ gives
$k\,(W - W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i]) \le \mathrm{In}_i + 2(k-1)L$; substituting
(3) of the manuscript and dividing by $k$ gives the claim, strictly. If $i$ has no
overtaker then $\mathrm{In}_i = 0$, the excess is at most $(2 - 2/k)L$, and the
bound holds strictly because $L > 0$ in the model of Section~3 of the manuscript.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~4 of the manuscript]
The argument for (3) of the manuscript gives
$\mathrm{In}_i < \mathrm{budget}(i,t) + kL$ at the instant $t$ of $i$'s last
overtaker, and $t \le s^P_i = a_i + W$ gives
$\mathrm{budget}(i,t) \le B_0 + \eta k W$, which is (5) of the manuscript.
Theorem~1 of the manuscript with $\mathrm{Out}_i \ge 0$ then yields
$kW - \eta k W < k W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] + B_0 + (3k-2)L$; divide by $k$. Both
inequalities are strict, by the same argument as Theorem~3 of the manuscript: when
$i$ has an overtaker the strictness comes from (3) of the manuscript, and when it
has none $\mathrm{In}_i = 0$ and the excess is at most $(2-2/k)L < (3-2/k)L$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why the Multiplicative Bound Is Claimed for One Shape Only}
\label{sec:supp_multscope}

With the queue-length shape, the multiplicative bound stated with one common constant is
false, which is why Section~5 of the manuscript restricts Theorem~4 to the age-relative
shape. The counterexample is below.

\begin{remark}[Why the multiplicative bound is claimed for one shape only]
\label{rem:mult_scope}
The same computation goes through when $B_0$ is replaced by a quantity
$B_{0,i}$ fixed at $i$'s own arrival, and then bounds $W$ in terms of
$B_{0,i}$. The queue-length shape of Section~5 of the manuscript sets
$B_{0,q} = B_0 + \gamma n_q$ with $n_q$ the number of jobs waiting when $q$
arrives, so $B_{0,i}$ varies from job to job. Stated with the common $B_0$ the
multiplicative bound is then false, by a margin that grows with $\gamma$. Take
$k = 1$, $B_0 = 0$, $B_{\max} = \gamma$, any $\eta \in [0,1)$, and the input: a
job of service $L$ at $t = 0$, which is dispatched at once; a second job of
service $L$ at $t = 1$, which waits with $n_q = 0$; the victim $i$, also of
service $L$, at $t = 1$ and ranked after it, so that $i$ arrives behind a single
waiting job, has $n_i = 1$ and hence budget $\gamma$; and $\gamma$ jobs of
service $1$ at $t = 2L$, all ranked above $i$. First-come first-served runs the
first two jobs on $[0,L]$ and $[L,2L]$ and starts $i$ at $2L$, so
$W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] = 2L-1$. Under the wrapper the second job is taken at
$t = L$ as well, by the guard if $\eta = 0$ and by the base otherwise; at
$t = 2L$ the victim has
$\mathrm{over}[i] = 0 < \gamma$, so the guard does not fire and the base spends
the whole budget on the stream, giving $W_P[i] = 2L - 1 + \gamma$ against a
right-hand side of $(3L-1)/(1-\eta)$. The bound is violated for every
$\gamma > (3L-1)/(1-\eta) - (2L-1)$, that is for $\gamma > L$ at $\eta = 0$, and
the margin grows without bound in $\gamma$. The same happens for
every per-job constant. For $\gamma > 0$ we therefore claim
Theorem~3 of the manuscript and not Theorem~4 of the manuscript.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Proofs of the Necessity Pair}
\label{sec:supp_necessity}

A work budget is necessary for a per-job guarantee, up to an additive $O(kL)$. The two
statements that make that argument, Lemma~2 and Corollary~2 of the manuscript, are
proved here.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~2 of the manuscript]
Every job $j$ counted in $\mathrm{Out}_i$ has rank below $i$, so first-come
first-served dispatches it before $i$ and $s^P_j \le s^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_j + G \le
s^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i + G$; it is also dispatched at or after $s^P_i$. All of them
therefore start inside a window of length
$w = G - \mathrm{excess}_P[i] \ge 0$. Of the jobs one server starts inside
such a window, all but the last complete inside it, so their total service is at
most $w$ and the last adds at most $L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~2 of the manuscript]
Theorem~1 of the manuscript gives
$\mathrm{In}_i - \mathrm{Out}_i \le k\,\mathrm{excess}_P[i] + 2(k-1)L$; add
Lemma~2 of the manuscript, whose hypothesis holds because the guarantee is
assumed for every job, and the terms in $\mathrm{excess}_P[i]$ cancel.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Floor of Order \texorpdfstring{$L$}{L}, and the Scope of Its Hypothesis}
\label{sec:supp_floorscope}

The floor of Proposition~2 of the manuscript is proved here, and its hypothesis is shown
to be neither droppable nor tightenable, the remaining cases of the budget family being
exhaustive.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~2 of the manuscript]
Into an empty system, at $t=0$, let $k+1$ jobs arrive together, all of service
$L$. The first $k$ ranks are precisely the jobs FCFS starts at once. Nothing has
completed, so every waiting job has $\mathrm{over}=0$; because its first-epoch
budget is positive, no waiting job fires and the wrapper accepts the base
policy's choices. At the first choice of a job beyond the first $k$ ranks, that
job displaces one of FCFS's first $k$ equal-length jobs. The displaced job waits
until a completion under the wrapper but starts at zero under FCFS, and hence has
excess $L$. A prediction-ranked base produces the same choice when one of the
later ranks receives the preferred score. At $k=1$ and $B>0$,
(3) of the manuscript gives $\mathrm{excess}<B+L$ strictly, and a family approaching
it is in Section~S8.4; $B=0$ is FCFS.
\end{proof}

Two remarks on the hypothesis, because the proposition is easy to state too
widely and too narrowly. It cannot be dropped: as a claim about policies the
statement is false, since first-come first-served is non-preemptive,
work-conserving, reads no service time before completion, and has excess $0$ on
every input, as does any rank-ordered policy. For the same reason it is false as
a claim about every base policy: the guard run around first-come first-served is
first-come first-served, whatever the budget. The force comes from the guard's
not firing, which is a property of the budget rule and not of work conservation,
together with a base policy that uses the freedom this leaves it.
It is also not necessary. A rule that is $0$ at the first dispatch epoch and
positive later, such as the age-relative shape with $B_0 = 0$ and $\eta > 0$,
falls to the same instance placed behind a saturating prefix: let $k$ jobs
of service $L$ arrive at $t = 0$, the victim just after them, and $k$ further
jobs just before $t = L$. The victim's wait up to $t = L$ is what first-come
first-served gives it, its budget at $t = L$ is positive because it has aged,
and the $k$ newcomers then take every server. The remaining case is degenerate:
if the rule gives budget $0$ to a job whenever no completed work has overtaken
it, then at every dispatch epoch the lowest-ranked waiting job $h$ has
$\mathrm{over}[h] = 0$, so $h$ lies in $E(t)$ and is its minimum-rank member and
is dispatched, and by induction the wrapper is first-come first-served itself,
whatever the base policy. For the budget of Algorithm~1 of the manuscript these cases
are exhaustive. If $B_{\max} = 0$ or $B_0 = \gamma = \eta = 0$ the budget is
identically zero and the wrapper is first-come first-served. If $B_0 > 0$ the
proposition applies, and if $B_0 = 0 < \eta$ the saturating prefix does. If
$B_0 = \eta = 0 < \gamma$, let $k+2$ jobs of service $L$ arrive together at
$t = 0$: the first has $n_q = 0$, hence budget $0$, and is released at once,
every other job has a positive budget and nothing has overtaken it, so a base
policy that prefers the highest ranks passes over the second job at $t = 0$ when
$k \ge 2$, and at $t = L$ when $k = 1$, and that job's excess is $L$. A budget
rule of this family therefore either makes the wrapper first-come first-served
around every base policy, or admits a base policy and an input with excess at
least $L$.

\subsection{Why the Budget Is Measured in Work and Not in Positions}
\label{sec:supp_workpositions}

Counting is not
necessary: a job can be passed any number of times and stay inside its promise, provided
the work that passes it is small. Counting is sufficient in this model, but only through
the worst-case charge of $L$ per overtake, since every overtaker is bounded by the time
limit; a count of $\kappa$ therefore buys a promise of $(\kappa + 2k-2)L/k$
(Remark~1 and equation~(2) of the manuscript). That is the bound a count can give, and it
is loose by the ratio $L/\bar{C}$, one to two orders of magnitude on our traces. Ten
overtakers that each run for the full limit $L$ and ten that run for ten milliseconds
carry the same count and delays that differ by three orders of magnitude, so the charge
is paid on every dispatch whether or not the work is there.
Section~8 of the manuscript runs the count rule as a baseline and measures how much
the looseness costs on a real load. That work is the currency the excess is actually
written in is the content of the identity of Section~6 of the manuscript, not a claim of
ours about what earlier mechanisms could not do.

\subsection{Which Assumptions Can Be Dropped}
\label{sec:supp_relaxations}

Four of the assumptions behind Theorem~1 and Theorem~3 of the manuscript can be
dropped, and one cannot. The global bound on service times goes first, and it is the
one whose replacement has to be read at the right instant.

\begin{proposition}[The prefix maximum replaces the global bound]
\label{prop:supp_prefix_max}
Write $\Lambda(t) = \max\{C_j : a_j \le t\}$ for the largest service time among the
jobs that have arrived by $t$. No global bound on service times is needed, provided
$\Lambda$ is read at the right instant, and the right instant is not the same for
every statement:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the upward direction of Theorem~1 of the manuscript, and hence Corollary~1 of
the manuscript, hold with $L$ replaced by $\Lambda(a_i)$;
\item the wrapper bounds (3) and (4) of the manuscript, and likewise (5) and (6),
hold in the sharp forms
$\mathrm{In}_i < B_{\max} + k\,\Lambda(s^P_i)$ and
$\mathrm{excess}_P[i] < B_{\max}/k + \Lambda(s^P_i) + (2-2/k)\,\Lambda(a_i)$, and
since $\Lambda(a_i) \le \Lambda(s^P_i)$ they give (4) with $\Lambda(s^P_i)$
throughout; with $\Lambda(a_i)$ in place of $\Lambda(s^P_i)$ both are false, and by
an unbounded margin;
\item the downward direction of Theorem~1 of the manuscript holds with
$\Lambda(\max(s^P_i, s^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i))$, and Corollary~2 of the manuscript holds
with $\Lambda(s^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i + G)$; both are false with $\Lambda(a_i)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Item 1 holds because every job counted in $R^P_i$, $R^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i$ or
$\rho^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i$ has rank below $i$ and so arrived by $a_i$. Item 2 needs the
later instant because the $kL$ term of (3) charges the last overtaker and the at most
$k-1$ overtakers still in service beside it, all of them jobs of rank above $i$, which
arrive after $a_i$ and are bounded only by the prefix maximum at the instant $i$
starts. The counterexample at the earlier instant is short. Take $k = 1$ with budget
$B = 2$, let $i$ and one job of service $1$ arrive at $t = 0$ and a job of service $M$
at $t = 1$, and let the base policy take the two others first. Then
$\Lambda(a_i) = 1$ for every $M$, the guard never fires because $\mathrm{over}[i]$
stops at $1 < 2$, and $\mathrm{In}_i = M + 1$ against a claimed
$B + k\Lambda(a_i) = 3$. In item 3 the horizon
$\Lambda(\max(s^P_i, s^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i))$ is not claimed to be the smallest that
works, and $\Lambda(s^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i + G)$ is what Corollary~2 needs because
Lemma~2 of the manuscript bounds jobs that start as late as that.

The remaining four are shorter, and the last of them is the one that fails.

\begin{proposition}[Relaxations]
\label{prop:relax}
In the setting of Theorem~1 and Theorem~3 of the manuscript:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Servers may have different speeds.} With speeds $v_1, \dots, v_k$
and $V = \sum_{\ell} v_{\ell}$, Theorem~1 of the manuscript holds with $k$ replaced by
$V$ on the left and the same $2(k-1)L$ on the right, and the guard satisfies
$W_P[i] \le W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] + B/V + (3k-2)L/V$: the budget is divided by
total capacity, not by the server count.
Here $L$ bounds a job's work in a common unit, not its wall-clock duration: a common
wall-clock limit does not produce policy-independent executed work when speeds
differ. The reference assigns each dispatched job to the fastest free server, ties
by server index.
\item \emph{The timeout is already in the model.} Section~3 of the manuscript defines
$C_j$ as executed work, $\min(C_j, L)$ for a job stopped at the limit, so every
currency above ($\mathrm{In}_i$, $\mathrm{Out}_i$, $\mathrm{over}[q](t)$ and
$L$) is in executed work and no assumption on raw service demands is needed.
Read in raw work the statements are false, a stopped job contributing more to
$\mathrm{In}_i$ than is ever executed.
\item \emph{Setup times survive.} If a setup of length $\theta$ is paid before
every service under both policies, absorb it into the job as
$C'_j = C_j + \theta$; the model is unchanged and every result holds with
$L \mapsto L + \theta$.
\item \emph{Work conservation cannot be dropped.} If servers may idle while jobs
wait, let $\Gamma^P_i$ be the server-idle time accumulated during
$[a_i, s^P_i)$ while at least one job waited. Then equation~(1) of the manuscript
becomes
$k W_P[i] = R^P_i + \mathrm{In}_i - \mathrm{Out}_i - \rho^P_i + \Gamma^P_i$, and
nothing survives without a bound on $\Gamma$: with $k = 1$, pausing the server
for $T$ while $i$ waits leaves $\mathrm{In}_i = \mathrm{Out}_i = 0$ and makes
the excess $T$. Release constraints are the same phenomenon: if rank is keyed
on arrival while eligibility starts later, the identity fails by exactly the
forced idle time, and ranking on release repairs it.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Only the last of the four changes what can be promised. The other three change the
constant, the unit or nothing at all.

\subsection{Replication, History and Aggregation Order}

Each overlay is built from the same \devnum{40} class-terms replicated
\devnum{44} times with independent whole-week shifts; each of the five overlays
therefore reuses the same jobs. Original scores are computed on the source calendar
and copied with their source jobs. Conservative histories are namespaced within a
class-term, use only replayed submissions and share the copy's shift. These copies
are not independently trained predictors or independent workload samples.
% Source: evidence/main_v3/out_main_v31.txt, trace construction table;
% prechecks/weakness1_attack/REPORT.md, development capacity experiment.

For the pending exact refinement, a copy means the same class-term, replication
round and shift. Its own submission outcomes follow policy-specific completions.
Other classes and terms are exogenous history in that copy's original relative
clock: when the target copy shifts by $s_i$, an external result is interpreted at
$\mathrm{done}_j+s_i$, not at another copy's independent shift. This preserves
earlier completed history without claiming a single platform whose copied users
share dynamically changing state. A separate sensitivity removes entire records
from other replayed classes in the same term, including arrival counts and elapsed
time since arrival; it then repeats same-copy refinement. Ordinary outcome
withholding preserves already-observed arrivals. The event order releases positive
duration completions before reads at the same instant, and zero-duration outcomes
after those reads. These conventions are specified in
\path{docs/policy_consistent_visibility.md} and implemented by
\path{src/spjf_guard/features/refine.py}.

In the package main and visibility tables, each overlay's deadline p99 is first
computed over its eligible jobs; its gap is then computed against that overlay's
FCFS and SJF. Reported p99 and gap are separate arithmetic means over overlays.
Consequently the displayed mean waits need not algebraically recover the displayed
mean gap. Harm and maximum excess are maxima over overlays. Firing is
queue-weighted within a cell, then averaged across overlays. Each bootstrap draw
uses the same week multiplicities for every policy and overlay, recomputes weighted
job quantiles and cell-level ratios, and averages the ratios. No table uses an
average of weekly p99 values. The capacity-sweep gap instead uses the ratio of
averaged overlay quantiles, as explicitly identified in Section~S6.

\subsection{The Selection Rule and the Cost per Event}
\label{sec:supp_rule}

The selection rule is stated here in full, with the data structures that make the
guard's firing test cost $O(\log n)$ per event, which is the figure Section~5.2 of the
manuscript quotes.

The rule is fixed before the grids are run: three
pre-stated grids, one per shape, \devnum{258} points in all; a point is feasible when
its harm is at most $G/2$ in every one of the \devnum{15} validation cells; the
objective is the deadline-window gap closed in the worst cell, with ties going to
smaller worst harm and then to smaller $B_0$, $\eta$, $\gamma$. Guard($G$) is the
winner over the three grids together; the winner inside each grid is reported as an
ablation row.

We keep
$\mathrm{over}[q] = TC - F(q)$ instead, where $TC$ is the total completed work and
$F(q)$ the prefix sum over ranks up to $q$ of completed work, maintained in a Fenwick
tree, so that a completion is one point update and a query one prefix sum. The firing
test is a segment tree over the window of waiting ranks supporting suffix range-add
and the query ``leftmost leaf with value at least $T$'', after rewriting
$\mathrm{over}[q](t) \ge B_{0,q} + \eta k (t - a_q)$, where $B_{0,q} = B_0 + \gamma n_q$
is fixed at $q$'s arrival, with $\eta k = e_n/e_d$ in lowest terms as
$e_n a_q - e_d B_{0,q} - e_d F(q) \ge e_n t - e_d\, TC$,
whose left side is a per-job value changed only by range-add at completions and whose
right side depends on $t$ alone. The capped channel needs only the head, since at a
fixed threshold $\mathrm{over}$ is non-increasing in rank. All budget arithmetic is
carried in integer microseconds, so the test is exact and no floating-point clock
enters the schedule. Each event costs $O(\log n)$ in the guard's own maintenance,
exclusive of what the base policy spends producing a proposal, and $n$ counts
waiting jobs, with arrivals sharing a timestamp processed in rank order.

\subsection{Why Open-Loop Benefit Has No Known Bias Direction}

The replay compares policies conditional on fixed offered work. Feitelson states the
objection at its strongest, that ``logged workloads actually contain a `signature' of the
logged system'' \cite{feitelson2021resampling}: a trace-driven simulation issues requests
irrespective of system state \cite{shmueli2009simulation}, so faster replies cannot
induce more submissions, change their costs or alter which jobs enter a deadline-window
quantile. Shmueli and Feitelson also distinguish increased productivity from improvement
in conventional waiting metrics \cite{shmueli2009simulation}. The two constructions below
show that the bias from the missing feedback has no known direction.
% Ninth pass: the framing above stood in Section 9 of the manuscript, which keeps the
%   statement that the results are conditional on fixed offered work.

In an exact
single-server feedback construction, jobs of length \devnum{10} and \devnum{1} s
arrive together, the long job first, and the short job's user submits a second
unit job \devnum{0.5} s after completion. Replaying the FCFS-induced arrivals
gives SJF a mean-wait advantage of \devnum{3.00} s; letting each policy generate
its own successor arrival changes the advantage to \devnum{-0.167} s.
The corresponding linearly interpolated p99 advantages are \devnum{8.82} and
\devnum{0.47} s. The job cohort is identical, so this example does not depend on
comparing different populations.
% Source: prechecks/weakness1_attack/out_feedback_counterexamples.txt, EXAMPLE 1,
% exact Fraction schedules and open_loop/closed_loop mean and linear_p99 gains.

The opposite bias direction is possible too. An initial FCFS order with services
\devnum{20}, \devnum{1}, \devnum{3} and \devnum{10} s, whose unit-job user returns
after \devnum{2} s with a \devnum{0.1} s successor, gives open- and closed-loop
mean advantages of \devnum{9.20} and \devnum{11.16} s and p99 advantages of
\devnum{10.00} and \devnum{10.18} s. These exact recursions falsify a general
lower-bound interpretation; they are not fitted behavioural models. The pathwise
theorem still compares a guarded schedule with shadow FCFS on that schedule's
realised arrivals and services, not independently evolving closed-loop systems
\cite{shmueli2009simulation}.

% parameters, exact schedules and gains; final assertion status PASS.

\subsection{LPC-EGEE: Recorded Queueing with Weak Counterfactual Validation}

The public LPC-EGEE trace supplies recorded waits and concentrated execution costs
together \cite{lpc_egee_trace}. After outage exclusion and queue-limit capping,
the longest \devnum{1\%} of \devnum{206{,}222} retained jobs carries
\devnum{40.7\%} of work. On the chronological evaluation part the top share is
\devnum{35.1\%}, which is the value used in the main cross-domain table.
The physical pools stay disjoint. Effective capacities \devnum{83} and
\devnum{56} are fitted only on the earlier part, with no test-driven refit.
Service is the minimum of recorded run time and its documented queue limit;
the common bound uses the largest pool limit, \devnum{259{,}200} s.
% Sources: prechecks/lpc_egee/applicability.csv, all/test aggregate rows;
% prechecks/lpc_egee/capacity.json and policy_parameters.csv.

The fitted FCFS approximation underestimates evaluation-part mean waits by
\devnum{24\%} and \devnum{39\%}, with hourly correlations of \devnum{0.44} and
\devnum{0.40}. The archive describes plain FCFS serial-job ordering during the
logged period, but queue concurrency ceilings, polling and middleware are absent
from the work-conserving replay. Recorded waits therefore cannot be equated with
the theoretical FCFS counterfactual. This is weak simulator validation, not
evidence that the original scheduler was an arbitrary non-FCFS policy.
% Source: prechecks/lpc_egee/validation.csv, fitted/test rows: mean ratios
% 0.757708/0.614029 and hourly Pearson 0.439849/0.397983; archive documentation.

In the conditional replay, SPJF-E reduces mean wait by
\devnum{21.4\% [13.2\%, 38.2\%]}, but its busy-hour p99 reduction of
\devnum{4.2\% [-11.2\%, 46.0\%]} remains unresolved. Busy hours use frozen
arrival-count thresholds, not deadlines. All six constant/age work-budget guards
at the tested multiples of $L$ fire zero times; a small fraction of the numerical
bound being spent is therefore not evidence of effective protection. Histories
use recorded completion times, and \devnum{2{,}906} evaluation arrivals
(\devnum{2.5\%}) have an outcome unavailable under replayed SPJF-E. This row is a
recorded-feature conditional counterfactual, not a closed-loop deployment result.

\subsection{Closed-Loop Replay with the Recorded Think Times}
\label{sec:supp_closed_loop}

judging trace one component of that feedback is recorded, and this subsection replays the

windows, with \devnum{11.7} s at the tenth percentile and \devnum{35.0} s at the

cost: the Spearman correlation is \devnum{0.015} over \devnum{2{,}000{,}000} sampled
result-gated pairs. The chains are \devnum{68{,}640} in number, \devnum{1{,}560} real
student-terms replicated \devnum{44} times across \devnum{17{,}634{,}760} jobs.

\section{Extremal Constructions and the Remaining Proofs}
\label{sec:supp_constructions}

Every constant the theory section calls exact is witnessed by a construction, and every
such construction is given here in full, beside the proofs whose ideas Section~6 of the
manuscript states. All of them are exact in integer arithmetic; every one was simulated
and every intermediate quantity of every proof checked job by job, with an independent
audit of work conservation and server capacity re-derived from the produced schedule. A
cross-reference without an S points into the main manuscript.

The six subsections below run in the order the theory section raises them, and each is
its own idea in one line. The cascade drives the workload gap of Lemma~1 of the
manuscript to its ceiling geometrically: in $m$ identical rounds, each opening with
$k-1$ long jobs of service $L$ and then $L$ short jobs of service $1$, a static-priority
policy that prefers the short ones carries $k-1$ part-served jobs into the next round,
and the carried workload obeys $\rho_{j+1} = (k-1)(\rho_j + L)/k$, so that
$\rho_m = (k-1)L(1 - ((k-1)/k)^m) \to (k-1)L$; the rescaling that fixes an arbitrary
service bound is part of the lemma. Both witnesses for the identity run that cascade
and then inject a finale at $T_m$: upward, $k-1$ long jobs, the victim and a stream of
more than $2kL$ unit jobs arrive together, first-come first-served starts the victim at
once with $\rho^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i = (k-1)L$ while the base policy holds it until the
stream runs out, leaving
$\Delta_i \ge 2(k-1)L - (k-1)L((k-1)/k)^m - (k-1)$; downward, the mirrored cascade is
followed by a filler job of service $f$, the victim and $k-1$ jobs of service $L$,
giving $\Delta_i = -\rho_m - (k-1)L \to -2(k-1)L$, and non-attainment follows from
Lemma~1 of the manuscript and the \emph{Bounding $\rho$} step of Theorem~1.
For the guard bound, part~(i) is a queue of $k$ jobs of service $L$, the victim and a
stream of unit jobs, where the constant budget $B$ makes the guard fire at
$L + \lceil B/k \rceil$, so the term $B/k$ is attained; part~(ii) bounds every finite
instance by Theorem~3 of the manuscript strictly and approaches $3k-2$ from below by
running the cascade for $m$ rounds and then injecting a finale timed so that
$\mathrm{over}[i]$ sits below $B = f + L + 1$ at three successive dispatch epochs, at
$0$, at $f$ and at $f + L = B - 1$, the last by a single unit, which lets the base
policy fill every server three times before the guard fires. The single-server family
places the victim ahead of $B/g - 1$ jobs of service $g$ and one job of service $L$,
with $g$ dividing $B$: the guard does not fire while $\mathrm{over}[i] = B - g < B$, so
the long job is taken and $\mathrm{excess}_P[i] - B = L - g$, which approaches $L$ and
never reaches it. The price of the guarantee counts inverted pairs in two ways, which
makes $\sum_i \mathrm{In}_i$ and $\sum_i \mathrm{Out}_i$ the two sums of service times
over the inversions exactly---the pairwise-interchange argument behind Smith's
rule~\cite{smith1956} stated on an arbitrary arrival sequence---and then bounds the
numerator through Corollary~2 of the manuscript and the denominator through the $mn$
inversions of shortest job first. Achievability counts the short jobs exact shortest job
first places before the first long one, which is
$r_g = \min(n, k\lceil B/(k\sigma)\rceil)$ and at least $qn$, while maximality turns on
one relabelling: if a wrapper permits a later-ranked job while the head has
$\mathrm{over}[h] > G - L$, giving that job the unrevealed size $L$ leaves the
transcript the wrapper sees unchanged and pushes the head past $G$, after which the
frontier counts $r = \lfloor (G-L)/\sigma \rfloor + 1$ permitted short-before-long
moves.

\subsection{The Cascade}
\label{sec:supp_cascade}

The cascade drives the workload gap of Lemma~1 of the manuscript to its ceiling, and
everything that shows the constant $2(k-1)L$ cannot be lowered starts from it.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:cascade}
Fix $k \ge 2$ and $m \ge 1$ and set $L = k^m$. There is an input and a
work-conserving policy $P$ such that, at time $T_m = mL$, first-come first-served
has completed every job and idles all $k$ servers, while $P$ holds exactly $k-1$
jobs in service, each with remaining service
\[
  f \;=\; \frac{\rho_m}{k-1} \;=\; L\Bigl(1 - \bigl(\tfrac{k-1}{k}\bigr)^{m}\Bigr) ,
  \qquad
  \rho_m \;=\; (k-1)L\Bigl(1 - \bigl(\tfrac{k-1}{k}\bigr)^{m}\Bigr) ,
\]
so that $U_P(T_m) - U_{\mathrm{FCFS}}(T_m) = \rho_m \to (k-1)L$ as
$m \to \infty$. Every $\rho_j$ and every intermediate time is an integer.
For any prescribed service bound $L_0>0$, multiplying every arrival time and
service time in the construction by $L_0/k^m$ gives the same ratios with bound
$L_0$; integrality is used only before this final rescaling.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The input consists of $m$ identical rounds; round $j$ occupies
$[T_j, T_{j+1}) = [jL, (j+1)L)$. At time $T_j$ inject, in rank order, $k-1$
\emph{long} jobs of service $L$ and then $L$ \emph{short} jobs of service $1$, so
that the long jobs carry the lower ranks. Let $P$ be the static-priority policy
that dispatches a waiting short job whenever one exists and a long job
otherwise; it is work-conserving.

The empty-system base round must be treated separately. At $T_0=0$ both systems
are empty. First-come first-served dispatches the $k-1$ long jobs and one short
job; its remaining server completes all $L$ short jobs by $T_1=L$, when the long
jobs also complete. Policy $P$ first processes the $L$ short jobs across all
$k$ servers, finishing them at $L/k$, and then dispatches the $k-1$ long jobs.
At $T_1$ each has $L/k$ remaining, so
$\rho_1=(k-1)L/k=(k-1)(\rho_0+L)/k$ with $\rho_0=0$.

For the induction step, fix $1\le j<m$ and suppose that at $T_j$ FCFS is empty,
whereas $P$ has exactly $k-1$ carried-over jobs with equal remaining service and
total $\rho_j$. FCFS again dispatches the new $k-1$ long jobs and one short job;
one server processes the $L$ short jobs and all work finishes at $T_{j+1}$.
Under $P$, the one free server takes a short job. The carried-over jobs complete
at $T_j+\rho_j/(k-1)$ and their servers then take short jobs. All $k$ servers are
busy until the short work runs out at $T_j+\tau_j$, where
$k\tau_j=\rho_j+L$. Indeed, $\rho_j/(k-1)\leq\tau_j$ is equivalent to
$\rho_j\leq(k-1)L$, which follows from the induction hypothesis. At that instant
$P$ dispatches the round's $k-1$ long jobs and leaves one server idle.

On $(T_j+\tau_j,T_{j+1}]$, FCFS has $k$ busy servers and $P$ has $k-1$.
At $T_{j+1}$ each of those long jobs has $\tau_j$ left, reproducing the
hypothesis with
$\rho_{j+1}=(k-1)\tau_j=(k-1)(\rho_j+L)/k$. Together with the base round this
solves to $\rho_j=(k-1)L(1-((k-1)/k)^j)$. With $L=k^m$, every raw $\rho_j$ and
$\tau_j$ for $j\leq m$ is an integer; the rescaling in the statement is then
applied to the whole input, including its arrival times.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Tightness of the Net-Overtake Identity}
\label{sec:supp_tight1}

In both directions $\sup|\Delta_i| = 2(k-1)L$, which is Theorem~2 of the manuscript. Two
witness families prove it, one for each direction.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~2 of the manuscript]
\emph{Upward.} Run the cascade of Lemma~\ref{lem:cascade} for $m$ rounds and, at
$T_m$, inject in rank order $k-1$ jobs of service $L$, then the victim $i$ of
service $L$, then $n_{\mathrm{sh}} > 2kL$ jobs of service $1$. Let $P$ continue as the static
priority ``the $k-1$ new long jobs first, then the short jobs, then $i$''.

At $T_m$ all $k$ servers of the reference policy are free and its waiting set in
rank order is the $k-1$ new long jobs, then $i$, then the short ones. It
dispatches the long jobs and then $i$, all in the phase at $T_m$. Hence
$W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] = 0$; the $k-1$ new long jobs are dispatched strictly
before $i$ in its sequence and are in service at $s^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i = T_m$
with their full service, so $\rho^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i = (k-1)L$ and
$R^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i = (k-1)L$.

Under $P$, one server is free at $T_m$ and takes a new long job; when the $k-1$
carried-over jobs finish, all at $T_m + f$ with $f < L$, their servers take the
remaining new long jobs and then short jobs. The victim waits while any short
job waits. Short jobs are executed at rate at most $k$, so they are not exhausted
before $T_m + n_{\mathrm{sh}}/k$, and $n_{\mathrm{sh}} > 2kL > k(f+L)$ makes
that later than $T_m + f + L$,
by which time every new long job has completed. So $i$ is dispatched after all
$k-1$ new long jobs have finished, and at $s^P_i$ the other $k-1$ servers hold
short jobs, giving $0 \le \rho^P_i \le k-1$. Also
$R^P_i = \rho_m + (k-1)L$, since the carried-over jobs and the new long jobs all
have rank below $i$ and the new long jobs are untouched at $a_i$. Therefore
$R^P_i - R^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i = \rho_m$ and
\begin{align*}
  \Delta_i \;&=\; \bigl(R^P_i - R^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i\bigr) - \rho^P_i
                 + \rho^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i
  \;\ge\; \rho_m + (k-1)L - (k-1) \\
  \;&=\; 2(k-1)L - (k-1)L\bigl(\tfrac{k-1}{k}\bigr)^{m} - (k-1) ,
\end{align*}
and both error terms are $o(L)$ as $m \to \infty$ with $L = k^m$.

\emph{Downward.} Run the mirrored cascade: inject the $L$ short jobs first in
rank order and the $k-1$ long jobs after, and let $P$ be ``long jobs before short
ones''. The induction of Lemma~\ref{lem:cascade} runs with the roles of the two
policies exchanged, so at $T_m$ the policy $P$ is empty and idle while the
reference holds $k-1$ jobs with $f$ of service left each. At $T_m$ inject, in
rank order, one \emph{filler} job of service $f$, the victim $i$ of service
$L-1$, and $k-1$ jobs of service $L$; let $P$ be ``the $k-1$ new long jobs
first, then $i$, then the filler''.

At $T_m$ the reference policy has $k-1$ servers busy with carried-over jobs,
each with exactly $f$ left, and one free server, which takes the filler---the
lowest-ranked waiting job---which also has service $f$. So all $k$ of its
servers complete simultaneously at $T_m + f$, and it then dispatches $i$, with
nothing else dispatched in that phase before it. Hence
$W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] = f$ and $\rho^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i = 0$.

Under $P$ the system is empty at $T_m$ with $k$ free servers, and it dispatches
the $k-1$ new long jobs and then $i$, all at $T_m$. So $W_P[i] = 0$,
$\mathrm{In}_i = (k-1)L$, $\rho^P_i = (k-1)L$, and the filler, of rank below
$i$, is dispatched after $i$, so $\mathrm{Out}_i = f$. Therefore
\begin{align*}
  \Delta_i \;&=\; k(0 - f) - \bigl((k-1)L - f\bigr)
           \;=\; -(k-1)f - (k-1)L \\
           \;&=\; -\rho_m - (k-1)L \;\longrightarrow\; -2(k-1)L .
\end{align*}

These calculations use the raw normalisation $L=k^m$. Given a fixed target
bound $L_0$ and tolerance $\varepsilon$, multiply the entire upward or downward
instance by $L_0/k^m$. The service bound is then exactly $L_0$, all waiting
times and every term in $\Delta_i$ scale by the same factor, and the upward
error becomes
$(k-1)L_0((k-1)/k)^m+(k-1)L_0/k^m$, while the downward error tends to zero.
Choosing $m$ large proves the statement with $L_0$ fixed.

\emph{Non-attainment.} For $k \ge 2$, Lemma~1 of the manuscript gives
$|R^P_i - R^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i| < (k-1)L$ strictly, and the \emph{Bounding
$\rho$} step of the proof of
Theorem~1 of the manuscript gives $0 \le \rho^P_i, \rho^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i \le
(k-1)L$; combining them in each direction gives $|\Delta_i| < 2(k-1)L$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Tightness of the Guard Bound}
\label{sec:supp_tight2}

Section~6 of the manuscript states that search does not see the growth of the additive
constant with $k$. A hill-climb over instances of at most \devnum{20} jobs suggests a
flat value near \devnum{$1.75L$}, while the witness below needs \devnum{396} jobs at
$k = \devnum{2}$.

%   the same one the table at the end of this subsection prints.

Both constants of the guard bound are exact, the first attained and the second
approached. The two constructions behind Theorem~5 of the manuscript follow.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~5 of the manuscript(i)]
At $t = 0$ let there arrive, in rank order, $k$ jobs of service $L$, the victim
$i$, and at least $B + k$ jobs of service $1$; let the base policy be the static
priority ``the $k$ long jobs, then the short ones in rank order, then $i$'', and
take the
constant budget $B$. First-come first-served dispatches the $k$ long jobs in the
phase at $t = 0$, filling every server, so $W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] = L$.

If $B = 0$ then $E(t)$ contains every waiting job at every dispatch epoch and the
wrapper is first-come first-served, so the excess is $0 = \lceil B/k \rceil$. If
$B > 0$ then at $t = 0$ no job has completed, $E(0)$ is empty, and the base fills
the servers with the $k$ long jobs. They complete at $t = L$, and
$\mathrm{over}[i](L) = 0$ still, every completed job having rank below $i$. From
$t = L$ on, all $k$ servers are free at each integer instant; while
$\mathrm{over}[i] < B$ the set $E$ is empty---the waiting jobs of rank above
$i$ have no completed job of still higher rank ahead of them---so the base
dispatches $k$ short jobs, which complete one time unit later. Hence
$\mathrm{over}[i](L + r) = rk$, the first instant at which
$\mathrm{over}[i] \ge B$ is $L + \lceil B/k \rceil$, and there $i$ is the
minimum-rank member of $E$ and is dispatched. So
$\mathrm{excess}_P[i] = \lceil B/k \rceil$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~5 of the manuscript(ii)]
For every finite instance, Theorem~3 of the manuscript gives
$c_{\mathrm{inst}}(k)<3k-2$: for a constant budget,
$k\,\mathrm{excess}_P[i] \le \mathrm{In}_i - \mathrm{Out}_i + 2(k-1)L
 \le \mathrm{In}_i + 2(k-1)L < B + kL + 2(k-1)L = B + (3k-2)L$, the last step
being the strict inequality (3) of the manuscript. When $i$ has no overtaker the
excess is at most $(2-2/k)L$ and the inequality is strict as well.

For the lower bound at $k = 1$, the family of Section~S8.4 gives
$\mathrm{excess}_P[i] - B = L - g \to L$, so
$(k\,\mathrm{excess}_P[i] - B)/L \to 1 = 3k - 2$. For $k \ge 2$, fix $m \ge 1$, $L = k^m$, and let $f$ and
$\rho_m$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:cascade}. Run the cascade for $m$ rounds. At
$T_m$ inject, all arriving at $T_m$ and in rank order,
\begin{itemize}
  \item $k-1$ \emph{new long} jobs of service $L$, of rank below the victim;
  \item the victim $i$, of service $L$;
  \item one \emph{sync} job of service $f$;
  \item one \emph{pre} job of service $L$;
  \item $k$ \emph{final} jobs of service $L$;
\end{itemize}
set the constant budget to $B = f + L + 1$, and let the base policy be the static
priority ``short jobs of the cascade, then sync, then the new long jobs and pre,
then the finals, and the victim last''.

\emph{The reference run.} At $T_m$ it is empty with all $k$ servers free; it
dispatches the $k-1$ new long jobs and then $i$ in the phase at $T_m$, so
$W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] = 0$.

\emph{The guard does not fire during the cascade.} Inside round $j$ the only
waiting jobs with completed higher-ranked work are that round's long jobs, whose
higher-ranked jobs are the $L$ short jobs of the round and everything in later
rounds, which have not arrived. Over $[T_j, T_j + \tau_j]$ the $k$ servers
execute $k\tau_j = \rho_j + L$ units of work, of which $\rho_j$ belongs to the
carried-over jobs, so at most $L$ units of short-job work can have completed.
Hence $\mathrm{over}[q](t) \le L < B$ for every waiting $q$ at every dispatch
epoch of the cascade, $E(t)$ is empty throughout, and the wrapped run reproduces
Lemma~\ref{lem:cascade}.

\emph{The finale.} At $T_m$ one server is free and $\mathrm{over}[q](T_m) = 0$
for every waiting $q$, so the base chooses and takes the sync job. The $k-1$
carried-over jobs have exactly $f$ left and the sync job has service $f$, so all
$k$ servers free simultaneously at $T_m + f$. There $\mathrm{over}[i] = f$,
because only the sync job has rank above $i$ among the completed jobs, and
$f < B$; no other waiting job has a completed higher-ranked job either, so $E$ is
empty and the base fills all $k$ servers with the $k-1$ new long jobs and pre.
These all have service $L$ and complete simultaneously at $T_m + f + L$, where
\[
  \mathrm{over}[i] \;=\; f + L \;=\; B - 1 \;<\; B ,
\]
since only sync and pre have rank above $i$ among the completed jobs. So $E$ is
still empty, the base fills all $k$ servers with the $k$ final jobs, and they
complete at $T_m + f + 2L$, at which instant
$\mathrm{over}[i] = B - 1 + kL \ge B$. The guard fires, $i$ is the minimum-rank
member of $E$, and it is dispatched with every server free, so $\rho^P_i = 0$.

\emph{The value.} $W_P[i] = f + 2L$, $\mathrm{In}_i = B - 1 + kL$,
$\mathrm{Out}_i = 0$, and
\[
  \frac{k\,\mathrm{excess}_P[i] - B}{L}
  \;=\; \frac{k(f + 2L) - (f + L + 1)}{L}
  \;=\; \frac{(k-1)f + (2k-1)L - 1}{L} .
\]
As $m \to \infty$ with $L = k^m$ we have $f/L \to 1$ and $1/L \to 0$, so the
ratio tends to $(k-1) + (2k-1) = 3k-2$. Hence the supremum, although no finite
instance attains it, equals $3k-2$.

The same instance also has $\Delta_i = \rho_m + (k-1)L \to 2(k-1)L$, so the two
sources of slack in Theorem~3 of the manuscript are saturated together.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Approaching the Single-Server Bound}
\label{sec:supp_floor}

At one server the bound $B+L$ of Theorem~3 of the manuscript is the exact supremum of
the excess, which is what the manuscript states in prose after Proposition~2. This is
the family that approaches it.

\begin{proof}[Proof of the single-server supremum stated after Proposition~2 of the manuscript]
Fix $L$, a budget $B>0$, and a granularity $g$ dividing $B$ with $0 < g \le L$.
Take $k = 1$ and, at $t = 0$, the victim $i$ of rank $0$, then $B/g - 1$ jobs of
service $g$, then one job of service $L$; let the base policy dispatch the jobs
of service $g$ first, then the one of service $L$, and never the victim.
First-come first-served starts $i$ at once, so $W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] = 0$.

Under the wrapper, after $r$ of the jobs of service $g$ have completed,
$\mathrm{over}[i] = rg$, which stays below $B$ for $r \le B/g - 1$. At the last
such instant $\mathrm{over}[i] = B - g < B$, the guard does not fire, and the
base dispatches the job of service $L$. When it completes,
$\mathrm{over}[i] = B - g + L \ge B$ and the guard releases $i$. Hence
$\mathrm{excess}_P[i] = B - g + L$, so $\mathrm{excess}_P[i] - B = L - g$, which
approaches $L$ as $g \to 0$ and never reaches it. For example
$(L, B, g) = (100, 40, 1)$ gives $\mathrm{excess}_P[i] - B = 99$, and
$(64, 32, 1)$ gives $63$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Price of the Guarantee}
\label{sec:supp_price}

A promise caps how much reordering can buy at all, which is Theorem~6 of the manuscript,
and at one server the achievable fraction is known exactly. At $k = 1$ the error term of
Theorem~1 of the manuscript vanishes identically and the sum identity is exact, so that
fraction can be computed rather than bounded.

\begin{proposition}[The achievable fraction at one server]
\label{prop:s_k1frac}
In the family of Theorem~6 of the manuscript with $k = 1$, the largest value of
$(\overline{W}_{\mathrm{FCFS}} - \overline{W}_{P})
 /(\overline{W}_{\mathrm{FCFS}} - \overline{W}_{\mathrm{SJF}})$
over all non-preemptive work-conserving $P$ with
$\mathrm{excess}_P[j] \le G$ for every job is exactly
$\min\bigl(1, \lfloor G/\sigma \rfloor / n\bigr)$.
\end{proposition}

%   ninth length pass folded into this file; its proof and the exhaustive check over
%   1,161 parameter settings were already below.  Nothing was recomputed.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~6 of the manuscript]
Let $\mathcal{I}(Q)$ be the set of inverted pairs of a policy $Q$, that is the pairs
$(u,v)$ with $u < v$ in rank and $v \prec u$. Counting the same pairs in two ways,
\[
  \sum_i \mathrm{In}_i \;=\; \sum_{(u,v) \in \mathcal{I}(Q)} C_v ,
  \qquad
  \sum_i \mathrm{Out}_i \;=\; \sum_{(u,v) \in \mathcal{I}(Q)} C_u ,
\]
exactly, for every $k$. This is the standard exchange argument: at $k = 1$,
where Theorem~1 of the manuscript is an equality, it says that the only way to
beat first-come first-served in mean wait is to invert pairs whose later-ranked
job is the shorter one, and that the saving is exactly the sum of the service
differences over the inversions. That is the classical pairwise-interchange
argument behind Smith's rule~\cite{smith1956}, stated here on an arbitrary
arrival sequence rather than on a single batch; we use it as an observation and
claim nothing new in it.
Write $S(Q) = \sum_i (W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] -
W_Q[i])$ for the total saving against the reference and
$\mathcal{E} = 2(1 - 1/k)L(m+n)$.
Summing Theorem~1 of the manuscript over the $m+n$ jobs gives
\begin{equation}
  \Bigl| S(Q) - \tfrac{1}{k} \sum_{(u,v) \in \mathcal{I}(Q)} (C_u - C_v) \Bigr|
  \;\le\; \mathcal{E}
  \qquad \text{for every } Q .
  \label{eq:sumbound}
\end{equation}

\emph{Numerator.} A pair contributes positively only when $u$ is a long job and
$v$ a short one, contributing $L - \sigma$. For a fixed long job $u$, the number of
short jobs dispatched before it is at most $\mathrm{In}_u / \sigma$, and
Corollary~2 of the manuscript gives $\mathrm{In}_u \le kG + (3k-2)L$. Summing
over the $m$ long jobs and applying \eqref{eq:sumbound},
\[
  S(P) \;\le\; \tfrac{1}{k}\, m\,(L-\sigma)\,\frac{kG + (3k-2)L}{\sigma}
        \;+\; \mathcal{E} .
\]

\emph{Denominator.} Sorting by service time inverts every one of the $mn$
long--short pairs, so its pair sum is exactly $mn(L-\sigma)$, and
\eqref{eq:sumbound} applied to it gives
$S(\mathrm{SJF}) \ge \tfrac{1}{k} m n (L-\sigma) - \mathcal{E}$. At $k > 1$ the
denominator carries the same error as the numerator, so treating the reference
saving as exact would understate $\varepsilon$.

\emph{Division.} Writing $\mathcal{N}$ and $\mathcal{D}$ for the two leading
terms $\tfrac{1}{k} m (L-\sigma)(kG + (3k-2)L)/\sigma$ and
$\tfrac{1}{k} m n (L-\sigma)$, both bounds are positive once
$\mathcal{D} > \mathcal{E}$, and then
\[
  \frac{S(P)}{S(\mathrm{SJF})}
  \;\le\; \frac{\mathcal{N} + \mathcal{E}}{\mathcal{D} - \mathcal{E}}
  \;=\; \frac{kG + (3k-2)L}{n \sigma} \;+\; \varepsilon ,
  \qquad
  \varepsilon \;=\; \frac{\mathcal{E}\,(\mathcal{N} + \mathcal{D})}
                         {\mathcal{D}\,(\mathcal{D} - \mathcal{E})} .
\]
The leading terms are
$\Theta(mn)$ and $\mathcal{E} = \Theta(m+n)$, so
$\varepsilon = O((m+n)/(mn)) \to 0$; since
$\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{D} = 2(k-1)L(m+n)/(mn(L-\sigma))$ and
$\mathcal{N}/\mathcal{D} = (kG+(3k-2)L)/(n\sigma)$, the product $\varepsilon\,mn/(m+n)$ equals
\[
  \frac{2(k-1)L}{L-\sigma}
  \Bigl(1 + \frac{kG + (3k-2)L}{n\sigma}\Bigr)
  \Big/ \Bigl(1 - \frac{\mathcal{E}}{\mathcal{D}}\Bigr) ,
\]
which exceeds the same expression without the last factor and approaches it as
$m,n$ grow. With $k,L,\sigma,G$ fixed it tends to
$2(k-1)L/(L-\sigma)$, and it vanishes at $k=1$.
Dividing numerator and denominator by $m+n$ turns the two total savings into the
two mean savings and leaves the ratio unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}
Write $r_u$ for the number of short jobs dispatched before a long job $u$. Every
short job ranks above every long one, so $\mathrm{Out}_u$ contains only long
jobs, and a long--long inversion $(p,q)$ puts $L$ into $\mathrm{In}_p$ and the
same $L$ into $\mathrm{Out}_q$; summing over the long jobs those terms cancel,
and since $k = 1$ makes Theorem~1 of the manuscript an equality,
\[
  \sum_{u \text{ long}} \mathrm{excess}_P[u]
  \;=\; \sum_{u \text{ long}} (\mathrm{In}_u - \mathrm{Out}_u)
  \;=\; \sigma \sum_{u \text{ long}} r_u .
\]
Let $u^{*}$ be the long job dispatched last among the long ones. It has no
lower-ranked long job after it, so $\mathrm{Out}_{u^{*}} = 0$ and
$\mathrm{excess}_P[u^{*}] \ge \sigma r_{u^{*}}$; and $r$ is non-decreasing along
the dispatch order, so $r_{u^{*}} = \max_u r_u$. The guarantee therefore forces
$\max_u r_u \le \lfloor G/\sigma \rfloor$ and
$\sum_u r_u \le m \lfloor G/\sigma \rfloor$. Only long--short inversions
contribute to the saving, each contributing $L - \sigma$, so
$S(P) = (L-\sigma)\sum_u r_u$ against $S(\mathrm{SJF}) = (L-\sigma)mn$ and the
fraction is at most $\min(1, \lfloor G/\sigma \rfloor / n)$.

For achievability, put $r = \min(n, \lfloor G/\sigma \rfloor)$ and dispatch the
$r$ lowest-ranked short jobs, then the $m$ long jobs in rank order, then the
remaining short jobs in rank order. Every long job then has excess exactly
$r\sigma \le G$, every short job has excess at most $0$, and the fraction is
$r/n$.
\end{proof}

So the shape of Theorem~6 of the manuscript is right at $k = 1$ and its additive
$(3k-2)L$ is slack: the guarantee is spent on the long jobs together, not one at
a time. Exhaustive enumeration of every work-conserving schedule of the family,
over all $1161$ parameter settings with $m \le 3$, $n \le 4$ and
$G \le 3L$, agrees with Proposition~\ref{prop:s_k1frac} in every case.

\subsection{Achievability and Maximal Permission}
\label{sec:supp_achiev}

The price bound of Theorem~6 of the manuscript is an upper bound on what any policy
with the promise can close. Two results say that the bound is nearly met, and that the
wrapper concedes nothing it could safely permit. Section~6.11 of the manuscript states
both in words; here they are in full, with their proofs. Neither gives instance
optimality on arbitrary inputs, and for $k\geq2$ the exact value of $F^*(G)$ below
remains open.

\begin{proposition}[Achievability on the two-size batch family]
\label{thm:two_size_achievability}
In the family of Theorem~6 of the manuscript, define
\[
  \Delta(Q)=\sum_i\bigl(W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i]-W_Q[i]\bigr),\qquad
  F(Q)=\frac{\Delta(Q)}{\Delta(\mathrm{SJF})},
\]
and let $F^*(G)$ be the maximum of $F(P)$ over all non-preemptive,
work-conserving policies satisfying $\mathrm{excess}_P[i]\leq G$ for every job.
Let the guard use exact SJF as its base and the constant budget
$B=kG-(3k-2)L\geq0$. Put
\[
 D_0=\frac{mn(L-\sigma)}{k},\quad
 E=2(1-1/k)L(m+n),\quad
 q=\min\!\left(1,\frac{B}{n\sigma}\right),\quad
 A=\frac{kG+(3k-2)L}{n\sigma}.
\]
If $D_0>E$, then
\[
 q-\varepsilon_-\ \leq\ F(\mathrm{guard})\ \leq\ F^*(G)
 \ \leq\ \min(1,A+\varepsilon_+),
\]
where
\[
 \varepsilon_- = \frac{E(1+q)}{D_0-E},
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_+ = \frac{E(1+A)}{D_0-E}.
\]
Thus the leading terms are separated by at most
$2(3k-2)L/(n\sigma)$, in addition to the two displayed finite-sample
errors. For $k\geq2$, this sandwich does not determine the exact $F^*(G)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{thm:two_size_achievability}]
For a policy $Q$, let
\[
 I(Q)=\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal I(Q)}(C_u-C_v).
\]
The summed identity \eqref{eq:sumbound} gives
$|\Delta(Q)-I(Q)/k|\leq E$ and, because SJF inverts all $mn$ long--short
pairs, $D_0-E\leq\Delta(\mathrm{SJF})\leq D_0+E$.

If $B=0$, the guard is FCFS and the lower bound is immediate because $q=0$.
Suppose $B>0$. Until the first long job is dispatched, every completed
overtaker is short and exact SJF continues to propose short jobs. They complete
in waves of at most $k$, so the number dispatched before that first long job is
\[
 r_g=\min\!\left(n,k\left\lceil\frac{B}{k\sigma}\right\rceil\right),
 \qquad \frac{r_g}{n}\geq q.
\]
Those $r_g$ short jobs precede every long job, and hence contribute at least
$m r_g(L-\sigma)$ to $I(\mathrm{guard})$. It follows that
$\Delta(\mathrm{guard})\geq qD_0-E$. Therefore
\[
 \Delta(\mathrm{guard})-q\Delta(\mathrm{SJF})\geq-E(1+q),
\]
and division by the positive lower bound $D_0-E$ gives
$F(\mathrm{guard})\geq q-\varepsilon_-$.

For any feasible $P$, Corollary~2 of the manuscript bounds the number of short
jobs inverted ahead of each long job by
$(kG+(3k-2)L)/\sigma$. Consequently its positive inversion sum is at most
$A kD_0$, and \eqref{eq:sumbound} gives
$\Delta(P)\leq A D_0+E$. Dividing by
$\Delta(\mathrm{SJF})\geq D_0-E$ yields
\[
 F(P)\leq\frac{A D_0+E}{D_0-E}
      =A+\frac{E(1+A)}{D_0-E}=A+\varepsilon_+.
\]
SJF minimises total waiting time for a simultaneous batch on identical servers,
so $F(P)\leq1$. The guarded policy is itself feasible by
Corollary~3 of the manuscript, which places it below $F^*(G)$ and completes the
sandwich.
\end{proof}

The guard therefore leaves at most $2(3k-2)L/(n\sigma)$ of the closable gap on the
table here, and at one server it leaves none of it. Saying so precisely needs the
class of wrappers the claim is quantified over.

\begin{definition}[Opaque-base, size-oblivious wrapper]
\label{def:opaque_wrapper}
At a dispatch epoch, the wrapper may use arrivals and ranks, its own dispatch and
completion history, the revealed sizes of completed jobs, the public bound $L$,
fixed random coins, and the sequence of proposals made so far by an opaque base
policy. It may not inspect unfinished job sizes or the base policy's internal
state. A deterministic pathwise promise $G$ means that every job has excess at
most $G$ for every finite input and every possible base-proposal transcript.
\end{definition}

Inside that class, and on an integer time grid, the guard's trigger is not one
admissible rule among several: it is the largest permission any wrapper with the
promise can grant.

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise maximality at one server]
\label{thm:k1_maximal}
Assume $k=1$, sizes and budgets are nonnegative integer time units bounded by the
positive integer $L$, and $G\geq L$ is an integer. Within
Definition~\ref{def:opaque_wrapper}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for constant budget $B\geq1$, the guard's worst pathwise excess is exactly
$B+L-1$; at $B=0$ it is FCFS and the excess is zero;
\item the largest positive integer constant budget with promise $G$ is
$B^*=G-L+1$;
\item at every reachable history, if the queue head $h$ satisfies
$\mathrm{over}[h]>G-L$, every wrapper with promise $G$ must dispatch $h$.
If $\mathrm{over}[h]\leq G-L$, permitting one currently waiting later-ranked
job for this dispatch is safe against the worst unrevealed size $L$.
Consequently the guard triggered by $\mathrm{over}\geq B^*$ is pointwise maximal
in its permitted action set.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{thm:k1_maximal}]
At one server Theorem~1 of the manuscript is the exact relation
$\mathrm{excess}_P[i]=\mathrm{In}_i-\mathrm{Out}_i$. If $B=0$, the queue head
always fires and the guard is FCFS. Let $B\geq1$. Immediately before the last
overtaker of a job is dispatched, completed overtaking work is an integer
strictly below $B$, hence at most $B-1$; the last unrevealed job has size at most
$L$. Thus every excess is at most $B+L-1$. Equality is attained by putting a
queue-head victim before $B-1$ unit overtakers and then one overtaker of size
$L$, and having the opaque base propose them in that order. This proves the first
claim and shows that a positive constant budget has promise $G$ exactly when
$B\leq G-L+1$.

For pointwise maximality, consider the minimum-rank waiting job $h$ at a
reachable dispatch history and write $w=\mathrm{over}[h]$. All lower-ranked jobs
have already been dispatched, so eventually $\mathrm{Out}_h=0$. If a wrapper
permits a later-ranked job $j$ when $w>G-L$, relabel the still-unfinished $j$ to
have size $L$ and replay the same opaque proposal transcript. The wrapper has
seen the same arrivals, dispatches, completions, revealed sizes and proposals,
so determinism makes it take the same action. The FCFS start of $h$ is unchanged
because $j$ has later rank, whereas
$\mathrm{excess}[h]=\mathrm{In}_h\geq w+L>G$, contradicting the promise.
Conversely, if $w\leq G-L$, permitting one later-ranked waiting job can add at
most $L$ to the head's overtaking work, so dispatching the head next remains
within $G$. On the integer grid, $w>G-L$ is equivalent to
$w\geq B^*=G-L+1$, exactly the guard's trigger.
\end{proof}

Read on the two-size family, that maximality fixes the exact fraction of the gap a
size-oblivious wrapper can close.

\begin{proposition}[The single-server consistency--robustness frontier]
\label{cor:k1_frontier}
On the integer two-size batch family, for $G\geq L$ the pointwise-maximal guard
around the exact-SJF proposal sequence closes, of the FCFS-to-SJF total-wait gap,
the fraction
\[
 \min\!\left(1,\frac{\lfloor(G-L)/\sigma\rfloor+1}{n}\right),
\]
and no opaque-base wrapper with the pathwise promise can permit a larger fraction
of those short-before-long moves.
\end{proposition}

Exact SJF itself reads unfinished true sizes, so the composed exact-SJF-plus-guard
scheduler is not size-oblivious; the frontier concerns the wrapper's permissions
under an externally supplied proposal transcript.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{cor:k1_frontier}]
With $B^*=G-L+1$, exact SJF proposes short jobs until the guard fires. The number
that can pass every long job is
\[
 r=\min\!\left(n,\left\lceil\frac{B^*}{\sigma}\right\rceil\right)
  =\min\!\left(n,\left\lfloor\frac{G-L}{\sigma}\right\rfloor+1\right).
\]
Each of the $m$ long jobs is inverted with exactly these $r$ short jobs, so the
closed fraction is $r/n$. Once their completed work exceeds $G-L$, the next long
job is the queue head and Proposition~\ref{thm:k1_maximal} forces every opaque-base
wrapper with promise $G$ to dispatch it; the same applies successively to the
remaining long jobs. No such wrapper can therefore permit more of the
short-before-long moves on this transcript.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fairness Referenced to First-Come First-Served}

Definition~2 of Schwiegelshohn and Yahyapour \cite{schwiegelshohn2000fairness} calls a
schedule $\lambda$-fair when jobs submitted after job $i$ cannot increase $i$'s flow
time by more than a factor $\lambda$. Their Lemma~4 bounds fairness and efficiency
jointly: the preemptive PFCFS policy is $(2+2\bar s)$-fair, with competitive bounds for
weighted completion time and makespan, where $\bar s$ is the normalised preemption
penalty, and the proof bounds the delay caused to $i$ by a later wide job by
$(1+2\bar s)p_i$. That is tighter for a short job than an $L$-denominated bound, and it
is obtained by gang preemption, which our model does not have.

In the fair-start-time line, Sabin, Kochhar and Sadayappan \cite{sabin2004fairness}
compare a job with a re-simulation excluding later arrivals, and Sabin and Sadayappan

use the resulting excess as a job-level metric. The fair start time is defined in two
variants; the reference used in the manuscript, first-come first-served with no
backfilling, is the relaxed one.

\subsection{Overtaking Restrictions in Queueing Theory}

Nudge \cite{grosof2021nudge}, Nudge-$K$ \cite{vanhoudt2022nudgek} and Nudge*(M)
\cite{charlet2026nudgestarm} are analysed in steady state under Poisson arrivals with
light-tailed or phase-type sizes, and yield, for suitable parameters, stochastic
dominance over first-come first-served. Because a job is passed at most once, Nudge
implies on every sample path a per-job additive bound of one small-job length; that
implication is our own reading and not a claim of \cite{grosof2021nudge}. Theorem~2 of
the WCFS finite-skip work \cite{grosof2022wcfs} brackets the mean response time within
additive constants of the M/G/1 first-come first-served expression at every load. Yu and
Scully \cite{yu2024boost} define the \emph{crossing work} of later arrivals that precede
a tagged job under $\gamma$-Boost and derive stationary response-time bounds, and Yu,
Harlev, Adakroy and Scully \cite{yu2025twotraffics} extend the line to $k$ servers.

\subsection{The Additive Shape, Stated as Its Sources State It}

Worst-case fair weighted fair queueing bounds a session's service in both directions
against the ideal fluid reference, falling behind it by at most one maximum packet and
running ahead of it by less than one of the session's own \cite{bennett1996wf2q}. The
error term in the packet scale rate guarantee for expedited forwarding is the
transmission time of one maximum-size lower-priority packet, and exists because that
packet cannot be preempted \cite{bennett2002psrg}; additive per-individual bounds
against a reference discipline are older still \cite{parekh1993gps,parekh1994gps}.
EEVDF, a preemptive proportional-share scheduler, bounds a client's lag against the
fluid ideal by $-r_{\max} < \mathrm{lag} < \max(r_{\max}, q)$, with $r_{\max}$ the
largest request and $q$ the quantum, and by $\pm q$ when no request exceeds a quantum
\cite{stoica1995eevdf}. In Graham's list-scheduling bound the additive form is
$(1-1/k)$ times the length of the last task to finish, and it appears inside his proof
\cite{graham1969bounds}. The pathwise work-difference bound nearest to Lemma~1 of the
manuscript is \cite[Lemma~2]{grosof2018srpt}, where the work of jobs of remaining size
at most $x$ under $k$-server preemptive shortest-remaining-processing-time exceeds its
single-server counterpart by at most $kx$; Lemma~1 differs in comparing two arbitrary
non-preemptive work-conserving $k$-server policies, in bounding total unfinished work,
and in being two-sided. In the conservation-law family a weighted sum of mean waiting
times is invariant across a class of disciplines in steady state
\cite{kleinrock1965conservation}, and the multiserver accumulating priority queue orders
service by a credit linear in waiting time, with exact class waiting-time distributions
under exponential service \cite{sharif2014mapq,li2016mapq}.

\subsection{Deployed Systems and Relational Benchmarks}

The judging-queue scheduler of Zhang, Wu and Lu \cite{zhang2024ojscheduling} cuts the
queue into fixed-size windows and dispatches in ascending predicted runtime within each;
it reports mean response time against first-come first-served, no per-job bound and no
adversarial-prediction test in its abstract or its results, and does not describe its
