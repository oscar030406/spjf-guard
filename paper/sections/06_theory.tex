\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

Everything in this section is stated for one fixed run: one arrival sequence, one vector of
costs and one realisation of whatever randomness the base policy uses. No stationarity, no
independence and no assumption about the scores enters a proof, and the upper bounds hold
even against a base policy that knows the future. An identity ties a job's delay relative
to FCFS to the work that overtook it (Sections~\ref{sec:th_gap}--\ref{sec:th_necessity}),
two bounds turn it into the guarantee of Algorithm~\ref{alg:guard}
(Section~\ref{sec:th_guard}), and three further results cover a maximum waiting time, an
absolute bound and completion reports that arrive late
(Sections~\ref{sec:clock}--\ref{sec:late}). Full proofs and the extremal constructions are
in Supplementary Sections~S4 and~S8.

\subsection{Conventions}
\label{sec:conventions}

We use the model of Section~\ref{sec:model} and fix three readings the proofs depend on.
The jobs started at one instant form a \emph{phase}, ordered as the policy starts them, and
$j \prec i$ refers to the concatenation of the phases. Within a phase, FCFS starts the jobs
in arrival order. A later-arriving job started in the same phase as $i$ but ahead of it counts
as overtaking $i$ and contributes its whole $C_j$ to $\mathrm{In}_i$, although it executes
nothing while $i$ waits. Remaining work at $a_i$ is read after the arrivals at $a_i$.

\subsection{The Workload Gap between Two Policies}
\label{sec:th_gap}

Write $U_Q(t)$ for the unfinished work at time $t$ under policy $Q$, counting every job
with $a_j \le t$.

\begin{lemma}[Workload comparison]
\label{lem:gap}
Let $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ be non-preemptive work-conserving $k$-server policies run on the same
input. Then $|U_{Q_1}(t) - U_{Q_2}(t)| \le (k-1)L$ for every $t$. For $k \ge 2$ the
inequality is strict, $|U_{Q_1}(t) - U_{Q_2}(t)| < (k-1)L$, and the constant is the exact
supremum, approached but never attained. At $k = 1$ both sides vanish, because one server
executes work at rate $1$ whenever at least one job is present, so every work-conserving
policy has the same unfinished work at every instant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}]
The difference $D = U_{Q_1} - U_{Q_2}$ is continuous and piecewise linear. Whenever
$U_{Q_1}$ exceeds $(k-1)L$, more than $k-1$ jobs are present under $Q_1$, every server of
$Q_1$ is busy, and $D$ cannot increase. Tracking $D$ back to the last instant at which
$U_{Q_1}$ was at most $(k-1)L$ gives the bound. Supplementary Sections~S4.1 and~S8.1 give
the proof, the strictness and the construction that shows the constant cannot be lowered.
\end{proof}

The argument that a work-conserving $k$-server system can fall behind only by what $k-1$
servers hold is the one used to compare a multiserver system with a single fast server in
the analysis of shortest remaining processing time (SRPT)
\cite{schrage1966srpt,grosof2018srpt}, where it bounds the work below a size cutoff. Here
we use it to compare any two non-preemptive work-conserving policies over all of the
work. FCFS also minimises the unfinished-work
vector in a stronger order, but only among policies whose assignment of jobs to servers
ignores their costs \cite{daley1987fcfs}, which the policies compared here do not.

\subsection{The Net-Overtake Identity}

For a job $i$ and a policy $Q$, let $R^Q_i$ be the remaining work at time $a_i$, under $Q$,
of the jobs that arrived before $i$, and let $\beta^Q_i$ be the remaining work at $s^Q_i$
of the jobs started before $i$ that are still running then. Write
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_i \;=\; k\bigl(W_P[i] - W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i]\bigr)
              \;-\; \bigl(\mathrm{In}_i - \mathrm{Out}_i\bigr).
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Net-overtake identity]
\label{thm:identity}
For every non-preemptive work-conserving $k$-server policy $P$, every input and every job
$i$,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_i \;=\; \bigl(R^P_i - R^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i\bigr) - \beta^P_i + \beta^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i ,
  \qquad
  \bigl|\Delta_i\bigr| \;\le\; 2(k-1)L ,
  \label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
with strict inequality for $k \ge 2$. Equivalently,
$\bigl|\,W_P[i] - W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] - (\mathrm{In}_i - \mathrm{Out}_i)/k\,\bigr| \le (2 - 2/k)L$.
At $k = 1$ the error term vanishes and $W_P[i] - W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] = \mathrm{In}_i -
\mathrm{Out}_i$ exactly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}]
Job $i$ waits throughout $[a_i, s^P_i)$, so every server is busy there and exactly
$k\,W_P[i]$ units of work are executed, none of it on $i$. Splitting that work by arrival
order gives
\begin{equation}
  k\,W_P[i] \;=\; R^P_i + \mathrm{In}_i - \mathrm{Out}_i - \beta^P_i .
  \label{eq:busy}
\end{equation}
The jobs counted in $\beta^P_i$ run on distinct servers other than the one that takes $i$,
so $0 \le \beta^P_i \le (k-1)L$. Under FCFS, $\mathrm{In}_i = \mathrm{Out}_i = 0$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} applied at $a_i$ bounds $|R^P_i - R^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i|$ by $(k-1)L$.
Subtracting the two instances of \eqref{eq:busy} gives the identity. Each direction uses
only two of the three ranges, which is why the slack is $2(k-1)L$ and not $3(k-1)L$.
Supplementary Section~S4.3 gives the proof in full.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A work budget is sufficient]
\label{cor:sufficient}
If a policy keeps $\mathrm{In}_i - \mathrm{Out}_i \le Z$ for every job, in particular if it
keeps $\mathrm{In}_i \le Z$, then $\mathrm{excess}_P[i] \le Z/k + (2 - 2/k)L$ for every job.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:sufficient}]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}, $k\,\mathrm{excess}_P[i] \le \mathrm{In}_i - \mathrm{Out}_i +
2(k-1)L \le Z + 2(k-1)L$. Dividing by $k$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

So a scheduler that never lets more than $Z$ units of work pass a waiting job holds every
wait within $Z/k + (2-2/k)L$ of its FCFS wait, whatever it ranks by. The constant cannot be
improved.

\begin{theorem}[Tightness of Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}]
\label{thm:tight1}
For every fixed $k \ge 2$, service bound $L>0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ there are inputs,
work-conserving policies $P$ and jobs $i$ with $\Delta_i > 2(k-1)L - \varepsilon$, and there
are others with $\Delta_i < -2(k-1)L + \varepsilon$. Hence $\sup |\Delta_i| = 2(k-1)L$, a
supremum that is never attained.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Theorem~\ref{thm:tight1}]
Both witnesses start from a geometric cascade that drives $U_P - U_{\mathrm{FCFS}}$ to
$(k-1)L\,(1 - ((k-1)/k)^m)$ in $m$ rounds and then place a victim so that FCFS starts it
at once while $P$ holds it back (upward), or the mirror image (downward). The instances
need hundreds of jobs and are rescaled to keep $L$ fixed. Supplementary Section~S8.2 gives
both in full. Non-attainment follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}.
\end{proof}

In the downward direction, half of the extremal value comes from the convention of
Section~\ref{sec:conventions} that charges a same-phase overtaker in full. Supplementary
Section~S4.3 gives the variant that charges executed work only.

\subsection{A Work Budget Is Also Necessary}
\label{sec:th_necessity}

A policy that guarantees anything about individual jobs is forced to bound the work that
overtakes them.

\begin{lemma}[Outbound work under a guarantee]
\label{lem:outbound}
Let $i$ be a job, and suppose $\mathrm{excess}_P[i] \le G$ and $\mathrm{excess}_P[j] \le
G$ for every job $j$ counted in $\mathrm{Out}_i$. Then $\mathrm{Out}_i \le k\,(G -
\mathrm{excess}_P[i] + L)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Lemma~\ref{lem:outbound}]
Every job counted in $\mathrm{Out}_i$ starts inside a window of length $G -
\mathrm{excess}_P[i]$, and of the jobs one server starts inside such a window all but the
last complete inside it (Supplementary Section~S4.7).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A work budget is necessary]
\label{cor:necessary}
If $P$ satisfies $\mathrm{excess}_P[j] \le G$ for every job on some input, then
$\mathrm{In}_i \le kG + (3k-2)L$ for every job $i$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:necessary}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:identity} gives $\mathrm{In}_i \le k\,\mathrm{excess}_P[i] +
\mathrm{Out}_i + 2(k-1)L$. Lemma~\ref{lem:outbound} bounds $\mathrm{Out}_i$, and the terms
in $\mathrm{excess}_P[i]$ cancel.
\end{proof}

The two corollaries say that bounding $\mathrm{In}_i$ and offering a per-job guarantee are
the same requirement, up to an additive $O(kL)$. A bound on the number of overtakes, as in
the mechanisms of Section~\ref{sec:related}, is not necessary for a guarantee, and it gives
one only by charging each pass the worst case $L$. That charge makes it looser than a work
budget by the ratio of $L$ to the mean cost, \devnum{150} on the main grading log ($L =
60$ s against a mean cost of \devnum{0.40} s). Supplementary Section~S4.4 proves both
halves and gives the conversion from a count to a bound.

\subsection{The Guarantee of the Guard}
\label{sec:th_guard}

Nothing below restricts the base policy, which may be randomised, learned or clairvoyant,
and nothing restricts the budget rule beyond the cap: it may depend on the job, decrease
over time, and take different values at two decisions made at the same instant.

\begin{theorem}[Guard bound]
\label{thm:guard}
Fix a base policy $A$, a cap $B_{\max} \ge 0$, and a budget rule that respects the cap,
that is, $0 \le \mathrm{budget}(q,t) \le B_{\max}$ for every waiting job $q$ and every
dispatch decision at time $t$, and run Algorithm~\ref{alg:guard} with that rule. For every
input and every job $i$, writing $W = W_P[i]$ for the wait under the guard,
\begin{linenomath}
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{In}_i &\;<\; B_{\max} + kL ,
  \label{eq:guardin}\\
  W &\;<\; W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] + B_{\max}/k + (3 - 2/k)L ,
  \label{eq:guardadd}
\end{align}
\end{linenomath}
where \eqref{eq:guardin} is asserted whenever $i$ has at least one overtaker.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Theorem~\ref{thm:guard}]
When the last overtaker of $i$ starts, $i$ is waiting and not eligible, so
$\mathrm{over}[i]$ is below its budget there. The overtakers started earlier and not yet
finished occupy at most $k-1$ servers, and the last one adds a further $L$, which gives
\eqref{eq:guardin}. Theorem~\ref{thm:identity} with $\mathrm{Out}_i \ge 0$ turns it into
\eqref{eq:guardadd}. Supplementary Section~S4.5 gives the proof in full.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The cap for a given bound]
\label{cor:params}
Setting $B_{\max} = k\bigl(G - (3 - 2/k)L\bigr)$ makes Algorithm~\ref{alg:guard} offer the
service guarantee $G$, whatever the budget shape below the cap and whatever the base policy
$A$. The choice is available when $G \ge (3 - 2/k)L$, and gives a positive budget only when
the inequality is strict. At equality $B_{\max} = 0$ and the guard is FCFS.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:params}]
Substitute $B_{\max}$ into \eqref{eq:guardadd}.
\end{proof}

The proof reads the budget only through the cap, so the shape below the cap is free. The
age-relative shape with one constant $B_0$ for every job also gives a multiplicative bound,
$(1 - \eta)\,W < W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] + B_0/k + (3 - 2/k)L$. The queue-length shape does
not, by a margin that grows with $\gamma$ (Supplementary Sections~S4.5 and~S4.6). Neither
constant of Theorem~\ref{thm:guard} can be improved: with a constant budget the term
$B_{\max}/k$ is reached exactly, and $(3-2/k)L$ is the exact supremum at every $k$,
approached by a cascade (Supplementary Section~S8.3). If the service enforces a run-time cap
known at arrival, charging it at the start and refunding the difference at completion
removes $L$ from the constant (Supplementary Section~S7.6). Finally, a guard that never
fires leaves the base policy's run unchanged, which can be checked on that run before the
guard is deployed (Supplementary Section~S4.2).

\subsection{A Maximum Waiting Time Gives the Same Guarantee}
\label{sec:clock}

Deployed schedulers guard against starvation with a maximum waiting time, and recent
inference servers promote a request once its wait passes a threshold
\cite{wu2023fastserve,luo2025autellix}, with no stated bound. In the simplest form, once
the earliest-arrived waiting job has waited $\theta$ it is started next, and until then the
base policy decides. We call this rule Timeout($\theta$). It reads arrival times and a
clock, never a cost.

\begin{proposition}[Timeout bound]
\label{prop:clock}
Fix a base policy and $\theta \ge 0$. For every input and every job $i$ with at least one
overtaker, Timeout($\theta$) gives $\mathrm{In}_i < k(\theta + L)$, and every job waits
\begin{equation}
  W \;<\; W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] + \theta + (3 - 2/k)L .
  \label{eq:clock}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Proposition~\ref{prop:clock}]
From $a_i + \theta$ on, the earliest-arrived waiting job has waited at least $\theta$, so
every job started before $s_i$ arrived before $i$. Each overtaker of $i$ therefore starts
before $a_i + \theta$ and runs at most $L$, and on each server the overtakers fit in
$[a_i, a_i + \theta + L)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:identity} with $\mathrm{Out}_i \ge 0$ gives the
bound (Supplementary Section~S4.11).
\end{proof}

At $\theta = G - (3-2/k)L$ the clock gives the bound that the guard gives at $B_{\max} =
k\theta$. Unlike Theorem~\ref{thm:guard}, the constant of Proposition~\ref{prop:clock} is
not shown to be tight: the worst ratio of excess to bound found by search is \devnum{0.996}
at $k = 1$ and \devnum{0.756} to \devnum{0.829} for $k = 2$ to $4$ (Supplementary
Section~S4.11), so Section~\ref{sec:exp_guard} also compares the two rules at equal
realised worst excess. The rules differ in what they charge a waiting job for. The clock
charges it for all the work the servers do while it waits, the guard only for completed
work of jobs that arrived after it. Near saturation, a job that arrives behind a backlog is
charged for the backlog, the clock fires for most of the queue, and the schedule falls back
to arrival order at the loads where ordering by cost pays most.

The two rules can be combined: start the earliest-arrived job if it has waited $\theta$,
and otherwise run Algorithm~\ref{alg:guard}. Both bounds then hold at once. The order
matters, since serving the guard's eligible job first can pass over a job that has
already waited $\theta$ (Supplementary Section~S4.11). The clock's half reads no completion
report, so the combined rule keeps \eqref{eq:clock} when reports arrive late or never.

\subsection{An Absolute Bound from the Arrival Log}
\label{sec:absolute}

A bound relative to FCFS is a margin over a schedule that is not run. The arrival log
alone gives an absolute bound, which an operator can state as a number of seconds. Let
$V_i^-$ be the work that a single server of rate $k$, fed the same jobs, would still hold
at $a_i$ from the jobs that arrived before $i$: $V_1^- = 0$ and
$V_i^- = \max\bigl(0,\ V_{i-1}^- + C_{i-1} - k(a_i - a_{i-1})\bigr)$. The largest value
$\sigma = \max_i (V_i^- + C_i)$ is the depth of the smallest token bucket of rate $k$ that
the arrivals, counted in work, conform to, which is the burst parameter of network
calculus \cite{cruz1991calculus,leboudec2001network}.

\begin{proposition}[Absolute bound]
\label{prop:absolute}
For every non-preemptive work-conserving policy and every job $i$, $k\,W[i] \le V_i^- +
(k-1)L + \mathrm{In}_i$. Hence $W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] \le (V_i^- + (k-1)L)/k$, and under
Algorithm~\ref{alg:guard} with cap $B_{\max}$
\begin{equation}
  W \;<\; \frac{V_i^- + B_{\max}}{k} + \Bigl(2 - \frac{1}{k}\Bigr)L
    \;\le\; \frac{\sigma + B_{\max}}{k} + \Bigl(2 - \frac{1}{k}\Bigr)L .
  \label{eq:absolute}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Proposition~\ref{prop:absolute}]
Equation~\eqref{eq:busy} gives $k\,W[i] \le R_i + \mathrm{In}_i$. A work-conserving
$k$-server queue never holds more than $(k-1)L$ above the single fast server: whenever it
does, all $k$ servers are busy and it drains at the same rate. Hence $R_i \le V_i^- +
(k-1)L$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:guard} bounds $\mathrm{In}_i$ (Supplementary
Section~S4.11).
\end{proof}

At $k = 1$ the fast server is the queue itself, $V_i^- = W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i]$, and the
proposition is Theorem~\ref{thm:guard} again. An operator who guarantees that no job waits
longer than $D$, over a period whose arrivals fit a bucket of depth $\sigma$, sets
$B_{\max} = kD - \sigma - (2k-1)L$: the capacity over the deadline, less the burst, less
$2k-1$ time limits. The clock obeys the same bound with $k\theta$ in place of $B_{\max}$.
Supplementary Section~S4.11 relates the statement to earlier sample-path comparisons with a
fast server, some of which were shown to be false \cite{wolff1987work}.

\subsection{Completion Reports That Arrive Late}
\label{sec:late}

Theorem~\ref{thm:guard} assumes that a completion is charged before the next dispatch
decision. In a distributed service the servers are separate machines that report their
completions to the scheduler over a network, and a server may take its next job before its
report has been counted. Suppose every report reaches the scheduler's counter within
$\delta$ of the completion, while servers still take a job the instant they free.

\begin{proposition}[Late charges]
\label{prop:late}
Under Algorithm~\ref{alg:guard} with a shared counter and charges delayed by at most
$\delta$, every job $i$ with an overtaker has $\mathrm{In}_i < B_{\max} + kL + k(L + \delta)$,
and
\begin{equation}
  W \;<\; W_{\mathrm{FCFS}}[i] + B_{\max}/k + (4 - 2/k)L + \delta .
  \label{eq:late}
\end{equation}
For $\delta > 0$ the bound is attained in the limit at $k = 1$ and at $k = 2$. If each
server applies its own completion report in the same step as its next pull, and only other
servers' reports travel late, Theorem~\ref{thm:guard} holds unchanged for every $\delta$,
lost reports included.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Proposition~\ref{prop:late}]
When the last overtaker starts, the charged part is below the budget as before. The
uncharged overtakers are those still running, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:guard}, and those that
finished within the last $\delta$ and whose report has not arrived. On one server the
latter are at most one job that started before that window and jobs that fit inside it, so
they hold less than $L + \delta$. When a server reports before it takes its next job, an
uncounted completion can belong only to a server that finished at the decision instant
itself and has not yet taken a job, so it holds nothing in service. Supplementary
Section~S4.12 gives both proofs and the instances that attain the bound.
\end{proof}

At $k = 1$ any $\delta > 0$ already costs a whole $L$, because the single server takes its
next job the instant it finishes one, before that job's report can arrive. The remedy is a
protocol change rather than a smaller budget: a server that counts its own completion
before taking the next job restores Theorem~\ref{thm:guard}, and the combined rule of
Section~\ref{sec:clock} keeps its clock bound for any delay.

\subsection{Which Assumptions Can Be Dropped}

We may replace the global bound $L$ by the largest cost seen so far, read at the right
instant. Servers may run at different speeds, with the budget divided by total capacity,
and a fixed setup paid before every job is absorbed into its cost. Work conservation cannot
be dropped. If a server may idle while jobs wait, the identity fails by exactly the forced
idle time. Supplementary Section~S4.10 states these relaxations with their sharp forms.

\subsection{A Floor of Order \texorpdfstring{$L$}{L}, and What a Guarantee Costs}
\label{sec:achievability}

No budget removes the last $L$ of the additive constant, because a job can be displaced
before anything has completed, and at that instant a guard that charges completed work has
nothing to charge.

\begin{proposition}[Floor]
\label{prop:floor}
Fix a budget rule that assigns a positive budget to every waiting job at the first dispatch
decision. Then there are a base policy $A$ that observes a job's cost only at its
completion, and an input, such that Algorithm~\ref{alg:guard} run around $A$ with that rule
leaves some job with $\mathrm{excess} \ge L$. Consequently no bound $G < L$ can be guaranteed by
a wrapper of this family that holds for every base policy, or for one prediction-ranked
base policy under arbitrary predictions, which is the generality in which
Theorem~\ref{thm:guard} is stated.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}]
Put $k+1$ jobs of cost $L$ into an empty system at $t = 0$. Nothing has completed, so no
job is eligible, the guard accepts the base policy's choices, and a base policy that starts
the last-arrived job in the first phase pushes back a job that FCFS starts at once, by $L$. Supplementary
Section~S4.8 gives the proof and shows that the hypothesis can be neither dropped nor
weakened.
\end{proof}

At one server the floor is the whole story: with a constant budget $B > 0$ the bound $B+L$
of Theorem~\ref{thm:guard} is the exact supremum of the excess (Supplementary
Section~S8.4). The guarantee also caps how much reordering can gain at all.

\begin{theorem}[Price of the guarantee]
\label{thm:price}
Take $m$ jobs of cost $L$ and $n$ jobs of cost $c < L$, the long ones arriving first, all
at $t = 0$, and let $P$ be a non-preemptive work-conserving policy with
$\mathrm{excess}_P[j] \le G$ for every job. Define $D_0 = mn(L-c)/k$,
$H = 2(1-1/k)L(m+n)$ and $\alpha = (kG + (3k-2)L)/(nc)$. If $D_0 > H$, equivalently
$mn(L-c) > 2(k-1)L(m+n)$, then
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\overline{W}_{\mathrm{FCFS}} - \overline{W}_{P}}
       {\overline{W}_{\mathrm{FCFS}} - \overline{W}_{\mathrm{SJF}}}
  \;\le\; \alpha + \varepsilon_+,
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_+ = \frac{H(1+\alpha)}{D_0 - H}.
  \label{eq:price}
\end{equation}
For fixed $k$, $L$, $c$ and $G$, the finite-sample term is $O((m+n)/(mn))$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Theorem~\ref{thm:price}]
Count the inverted pairs of long and short jobs in two ways, once through the mean waits,
which reads the interchange argument behind Smith's rule \cite{smith1956} on this input,
and once through Corollary~\ref{cor:necessary} applied to each long job. Supplementary
Section~S8.5 gives the proof.
\end{proof}

The bound is capacity times $G$, divided by the work that wants to overtake. It is
linear in $G$ and reaches $1$ when $kG$ covers the total cost of the short jobs, and
because it applies to every policy with the guarantee, it describes the trade-off an
operator faces rather than a weakness of this guard. At $k = 1$ the achievable fraction is
known exactly, $\lfloor G/c \rfloor / n$ capped at $1$, and the guard around exact SJF
meets the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:price} on this family up to $2(3k-2)L/(nc)$ and
finite-sample terms
(Supplementary Sections~S8.5 and~S8.6, with an exhaustive check over \devnum{1{,}161}
parameter settings).
