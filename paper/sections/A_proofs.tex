\subsection{The workload comparison}
\label{app:strict}

% Sixth/seventh pass, referee item G3: the two proofs that stood here in full are now
% Supplementary Section S4.1.  What is left is the proof idea and the pointer; nothing
% in either argument changed.
Section~\ref{sec:theory} gives the idea of the bound. The strictness at $k \ge 2$
runs the same way: at the first instant $t_1$ at which $D = U_{Q_1} - U_{Q_2}$
touches $(k-1)L$, the linear piece to its left must have positive slope, which
forces at most $k-1$ jobs present under $Q_1$ there, so none of them waits and
$U_{Q_1} \le (k-1)L$ on that piece; left limits then force $U_{Q_2}(t_1^-) = 0$ and
$U_{Q_1}(t_1^-) = (k-1)L$, and $U_{Q_1}$ is strictly decreasing on the piece, so it
cannot equal its own supremum at $t_1$. Both steps invoke $(k-1)L > 0$ and both fail
at $k = 1$, where $U_{Q_1} \equiv U_{Q_2}$ and the bound $0$ is attained everywhere.
Supplementary Section~S4.1 gives both proofs in full; that the constant cannot be
lowered is the cascade of Appendix~\ref{app:cascade}.

\subsection{The cascade}
\label{app:cascade}

Both tightness results begin from the same construction, which drives the
workload gap of Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} to its ceiling geometrically. In $m$ identical
rounds, each opening with $k-1$ long jobs of service $L$ and then $L$ short jobs of
service $1$, a static-priority policy that prefers the short ones carries $k-1$
part-served jobs into the next round, and the carried workload obeys
$\rho_{j+1} = (k-1)(\rho_j + L)/k$, so that
$\rho_m = (k-1)L(1 - ((k-1)/k)^m) \to (k-1)L$. Supplementary Section~S8.1 states the
cascade lemma and proves it, including the rescaling that fixes an arbitrary service
bound.



\subsection{Tightness of the net-overtake identity}
\label{app:tight1}

Both witnesses run the cascade for $m$ rounds and then inject a finale at $T_m$.
Upward, $k-1$ long jobs, the victim and a stream of more than $2kL$ unit jobs arrive
together: first-come first-served starts the victim at once with
$\rho^{\mathrm{FCFS}}_i = (k-1)L$, while the base policy holds it until the stream
runs out, which leaves
$\Delta_i \ge 2(k-1)L - (k-1)L((k-1)/k)^m - (k-1)$. Downward, the mirrored cascade is
followed by a filler job of service $f$, the victim and $k-1$ jobs of service $L$,
giving $\Delta_i = -\rho_m - (k-1)L \to -2(k-1)L$. Non-attainment follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} and the \emph{Bounding $\rho$} step of
Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}. Supplementary Section~S8.2 gives both constructions in
full, with the table of the exact values of $\Delta_i/L$ they attain at
$k = \devnum{2}, \devnum{3}, \devnum{4}$, the executed-work column, the same-phase
shares and the victims' own excesses.



\subsection{Tightness of the guard bound}
\label{app:tight2}

Part~(i) is a queue of $k$ jobs of service $L$, the victim and a stream of unit jobs:
with the constant budget $B$ the guard fires at $L + \lceil B/k \rceil$, so the term
$B/k$ is attained. Part~(ii) bounds every finite instance by
Theorem~\ref{thm:guard} strictly, and approaches $3k-2$ from below by running the
cascade for $m$ rounds and then injecting a finale timed so that
$\mathrm{over}[i]$ sits below $B = f + L + 1$ at three successive dispatch epochs,
at $0$, at $f$ and at $f + L = B - 1$, the last by a single unit, which lets the base
policy fill every server three times before the guard fires. Supplementary
Section~S8.3 gives both proofs and the table of the values the family reaches, which
needs \devnum{396} jobs at $k = \devnum{2}$.

\subsection{Approaching the single-server bound}
\label{app:floor}

At $k = 1$, with budget $B > 0$ and a granularity $g$ dividing $B$, the victim is
placed ahead of $B/g - 1$ jobs of service $g$ and one job of service $L$: the guard
does not fire while $\mathrm{over}[i] = B - g < B$, so the long job is taken and
$\mathrm{excess}_P[i] - B = L - g$, which approaches $L$ and never reaches it.
Supplementary Section~S8.4 gives the family in full with two worked settings.



\subsection{The price of the guarantee}
\label{app:price}

Counting inverted pairs in two ways makes $\sum_i \mathrm{In}_i$ and
$\sum_i \mathrm{Out}_i$ the two sums of service times over the inversions exactly,
which is the pairwise-interchange argument behind Smith's rule~\cite{smith1956}
stated on an arbitrary arrival sequence; summing Theorem~\ref{thm:identity} over the
$m+n$ jobs then brackets the total saving of any policy by the inversion sum divided
by $k$, to within $\mathcal{E} = 2(1-1/k)L(m+n)$. Bounding the numerator through
Corollary~\ref{cor:necessary} and the denominator through the $mn$ inversions of
shortest job first gives the ratio, with the finite-sample term
$\varepsilon = O((m+n)/(mn))$. Supplementary Section~S8.5 gives the proof in full.



At $k = 1$ the error term vanishes identically and the sum identity is exact, so
the achievable fraction can be computed rather than bounded.

\begin{proposition}[The achievable fraction at one server]
\label{prop:k1frac}
In the family of Theorem~\ref{thm:price} with $k = 1$, the largest value of
$(\overline{W}_{\mathrm{FCFS}} - \overline{W}_{P})
 /(\overline{W}_{\mathrm{FCFS}} - \overline{W}_{\mathrm{SJF}})$
over all non-preemptive work-conserving $P$ with
$\mathrm{excess}_P[j] \le G$ for every job is exactly
$\min\bigl(1, \lfloor G/\sigma \rfloor / n\bigr)$.
\end{proposition}

Supplementary Section~S8.5 proves it and reports the exhaustive check of every
work-conserving schedule of the family over \devnum{1{,}161} parameter settings.



\subsection{Achievability and maximal permission}
\label{app:achievability}

The lower bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:two_size_achievability} counts the short jobs
exact shortest job first places before the first long one, which is
$r_g = \min(n, k\lceil B/(k\sigma)\rceil)$ and at least $qn$; the upper bound applies
Corollary~\ref{cor:necessary} to each long job. Theorem~\ref{thm:k1_maximal} turns on
one relabelling: if a wrapper permits a later-ranked job while the head has
$\mathrm{over}[h] > G - L$, giving that job the unrevealed size $L$ leaves the
transcript the wrapper sees unchanged and pushes the head past $G$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:k1_frontier} then counts $r = \lfloor (G-L)/\sigma \rfloor + 1$
permitted short-before-long moves. Supplementary Section~S8.6 gives the three proofs
in full.
